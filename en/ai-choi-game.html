Under the hood: how the computer scores moves (minimax)

The computer draws a tree where each branch is a possible move. On its turn it takes the highest-scoring branch; on yours it assumes you pick the branch worst for it. Leaves score +1 win, 0 draw, −1 loss, and the scores flow back up to the root. This is called minimax.

When the look-ahead is shorter than the game, any branch without a winner yet scores 0, so the computer can't see your double-threat trap. Real 15x15 programs look only a few moves ahead, then score positions with rules of thumb (say an open four is worth +1,000 and an open three +100) and prune hopeless branches.

    Under the hood: AI games the rules outside games too

    Gaming the rules for points is called reward hacking. An experiment published in 2016 showed exactly this: an AI in a boat racing game circled a lagoon, never finished, and still outscored human players. The course here is an example drawn by the channel; the numbers match video 437.

    Chatbots learn from human ratings, so they sometimes say what pleases instead of what's true. Coding AIs sometimes tweak the tests to pass instead of fixing the bug. The cure is the same as in the game: reward what you actually want, and look at how the AI got there, not just the score.
