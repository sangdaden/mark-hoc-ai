Part 1

Piece #9 showed CRISPR scissors treating one person at a time. Used on an embryo, though, the change reaches the children and grandchildren too. Who gets to decide that, and what happens when one person decides for the whole world?

Part 2

The story

Who, where, when. The team never published a peer-reviewed paper, so almost every detail comes from the press and the court ruling.

2015 · The first experiment on human embryos, in the labHuang Junjiu's team (Guangzhou) tried CRISPR on human embryos that could not develop, targeting the gene behind beta thalassaemia. Result: poor repair, "mosaic" embryos (edited and unedited cells mixed), off-target cuts. Their conclusion: far from ready for people.
26 Nov 2018 · A YouTube videoHe Jiankui, a researcher in Shenzhen, announced twin girls (pseudonyms Lulu and Nana) born a few weeks earlier from embryos whose CCR5 gene, the door HIV uses to enter cells, had been edited. The father had HIV.
28 Nov 2018 · The Hong Kong summitHe presented at the Second International Summit on Human Genome Editing. Scientists asked: where is the medical need, who did the ethics review, where are the data? The answers did not convince.
Late Nov 2018 · HaltedChinese authorities halted the team's research and opened an investigation.
Dec 2019 · The manuscript surfacesMIT Technology Review published excerpts from the manuscript major journals had rejected. The babies did not carry the natural HIV-resistance variant, but new variants with unknown effects.
30 Dec 2019 · The verdictA Shenzhen court sentenced He Jiankui to 3 years in prison and a 3 million yuan fine for illegal medical practice; two collaborators were also convicted. The court said the team had forged ethics review documents, and confirmed a third baby.
Apr 2022 · ReleasedHe Jiankui finished his sentence. The three children's identities remain protected.

Source: Liang et al. (2015), Protein & Cell 6(5): 363–372; AP, STAT, NPR 26 Nov 2018; Hong Kong summit via NPR and GEN; research halt per Xinhua (via press); MIT Technology Review Dec 2019 (manuscript excerpts); ruling of the Nanshan District court, Shenzhen, 30 Dec 2019 via Xinhua, AP, Bloomberg; MIT Technology Review 4 Apr 2022 (release). Except the 2015 paper, all are secondary sources.

Why did scientists call the experiment "unnecessary"?

To stop a father with HIV from passing it on, medicine already had "sperm washing" before IVF. David Liu and Jennifer Doudna, two of the people behind gene-editing tools, both made this point: there was no unmet medical need.

In other words, healthy babies bore the risk of an untested experiment for a benefit that could be had more safely.

Source: remarks by David Liu and Jennifer Doudna, via GEN and the South China Morning Post, Nov 2018 (secondary sources).

Part 3

How the gene works

1. Editing body cells (like the medicine in piece #9) affects one person. Editing a one-cell embryo puts the change into every cell, including the future eggs or sperm, so it passes to descendants.

2. The CCR5 gene makes a "door" on white blood cells that most HIV strains use to get in. Some people naturally carry the Δ32 variant (32 letters missing), which keeps the door shut.

3. Scissors in an embryo cannot be fully controlled: each cell may repair differently, making a "mosaic" embryo, and new variants can appear instead of Δ32.

SOLIDIn Nov 2018, He Jiankui announced twin girls born from edited embryos

Announcement video 26 Nov 2018 (AP, STAT); the 2019 ruling confirmed the births.

SOLIDThe aim was to disable CCR5 against HIV; the father had HIV

As He told AP and NPR (secondary).

SOLIDThere was already a way to avoid passing HIV from father to child without gene editing

"Sperm washing" before IVF; raised by David Liu and Jennifer Doudna at the time (GEN, SCMP; secondary).

SOLIDA Shenzhen court gave 3 years in prison and a 3 million yuan fine

On 30 Dec 2019, for illegal medical practice; Zhang Renli 2 years, Qin Jinzhou an 18-month suspended sentence (Xinhua via AP, Bloomberg; secondary).

SOLIDThere was a third gene-edited baby

The ruling said the team was involved in three births to two mothers (Xinhua via AP).

SOLIDThe team forged ethics review documents

Per the court ruling, via Xinhua and AP (secondary).

SOLIDWHO issued a global governance framework and recommendations in 2021

WHO release 12 July 2021: a governance framework with illustrative scenarios, plus recommendations on research registries, medical travel and education. The expert committee was set up in Dec 2018, right after the case.

SOLIDInternational science bodies concluded heritable editing is not acceptable for now

International Commission of the US academies and the UK Royal Society (3 Sep 2020): not ready; Third International Summit, London (8 Mar 2023): "remains unacceptable at this time".

CAUTION"At least one baby is mosaic"

Scientists who saw the documents told AP so; the full data were never published for independent checking.

CAUTION"The children are perfectly healthy, nothing to worry about"

No independent medical data have been published. Their identities are protected, rightly, which also means nobody outside knows for sure.

FALSE"He recreated the natural HIV-resistance variant"

Manuscript excerpts: the right gene, CCR5, but new variants, not Δ32. Fyodor Urnov called the claim a misrepresentation of the data (MIT Technology Review Dec 2019; secondary).

FALSE"He was the first to use CRISPR on human embryos"

In 2015, Huang Junjiu's team had tried it on non-viable embryos in the lab (Liang et al., Protein & Cell 2015). He was the first to announce children born from edited embryos.

FALSE"Science proved that CCR5-Δ32 carriers die early"

A 2019 Nature Medicine paper said so, but its authors retracted it over an error in the UK Biobank data; a 2020 reanalysis found no effect on lifespan.

FALSE"After this, nobody wants to edit human embryos any more"

In 2025 at least two US start-ups (Preventive, Manhattan Genomics) raised money to research embryo editing; many scientists object (MIT Technology Review 31 Oct 2025; secondary).

Part 5

Where we are today

After 2018 the world put up fences. But the door is not fully shut.

3 Sep 2020 · International CommissionSet up by the US National Academy of Medicine, National Academy of Sciences and the UK Royal Society. Conclusion: edited embryos must not be used for pregnancy, as no technology can yet make precise changes without unwanted ones. If ever allowed, start only with serious single-gene diseases.
12 July 2021 · WHOThe first global governance framework and recommendations on human genome editing, including a registry of human genome editing studies and the issue of medical travel. It repeated: now is not the time for heritable editing.
8 Mar 2023 · London summitThe Third International Summit: heritable editing "remains unacceptable at this time"; it also noted that somatic editing has cured diseases and called for affordable access.
8 July 2024 · ChinaThe Ministry of Science and Technology's ethics guidelines: any clinical research on germline editing "is currently irresponsible and not allowed"; using edited embryos for pregnancy is banned.
Oct 2025 · Start-upsA US company has about 30 million USD to research embryo editing and says it will not rush. Many scientists call the direction dangerous and a distraction from the real benefits of gene editing for patients (secondary).
In EuropeThe Oviedo Convention (1997) bans interventions aimed at changing the genome of descendants; 30 states have ratified it.

Source: release of the US National Academies and the UK Royal Society, 3 Sep 2020; WHO 12 July 2021; statement of the Organising Committee of the Third International Summit, Royal Society 8 Mar 2023; guidelines of China's Ministry of Science and Technology, 8 July 2024, via Xinhua and gov.cn; Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe (PACE) 2023 report on Article 13 of the Oviedo Convention; MIT Technology Review 31 Oct 2025 (secondary). Label: FALSE, ethically and scientifically. Fast does not mean right; and a good tool (piece #9) can still be misused.

More: why does Mark rate the "response" 4/5 rather than 5/5?

Scientists reacted quickly and set out clear guidance. But the experiment went all the way to births without anyone outside the team stopping it; at the Hong Kong summit itself, David Baltimore called that a failure of self-regulation, due to a lack of transparency.

And by 2025, embryo editing was being discussed again by private companies. The fences exist, but they need guarding.

The "More" section is Mark's commentary; "failure of self-regulation" is David Baltimore's phrase at the Hong Kong summit, via NPR (secondary).

Part 7

Lessons: what to ask before believing, and before taking part

Three children had no choice. Adults can ask.

For reading and understanding, not a substitute for medical advice.

1 · Is there a real need?Is there already a safer way? If so, why try a new one?
2 · Who reviewed it?Did an independent ethics committee approve the study? Is it publicly registered?
3 · Fully explained?Risks, alternatives, the right to withdraw: were they written and explained before signing?
4 · Peer-reviewed paper?A "breakthrough" with only a video or a press release is not yet evidence.
Beware offers to "edit your baby's genes"Editing embryos for pregnancy is banned in many countries and judged unacceptable by WHO and the academies. If someone offers it, check with a genetic doctor.
Want to know your inherited risk?The question to ask is: "Does my family need genetic counselling or carrier testing?" (as with thalassaemia in piece #5).
Self-check: rereading the 2018 case with the four questions

1. Real need? No: sperm washing existed.

2. Who reviewed it? The court found the ethics documents forged.

3. Fully explained? Cannot be verified; and the babies could not consent.

4. Peer-reviewed paper? None; only a video and a rejected manuscript.

Four questions, four "no"s. Next time you read a "breakthrough", count how many "yes"es it gets.

The four questions are Mark's suggestions, based on principles in the WHO governance framework (2021) and the facts in Part 2.
