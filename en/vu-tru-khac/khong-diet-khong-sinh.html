Part 1

Core thesis

We fear death because we believe we came from nothing into being and will return from being to nothing. Looking deeply, nothing arises from nothing or vanishes into nothing; there is only manifestation and continuation. See that, and fear dissolves.

Step 1The root of fearThe notion that birth is "nothing becoming something" and death is "becoming nothing".
→
Step 2Look deeplyClouds, waves, a match flame: things manifest when conditions are sufficient, then go hidden.
→
Step 3PractiseBreathe, contemplate, see loved ones in yourself. Understand by experience, not only by idea.

Part 4

How well do the claims and images hold up?

This is a spiritual book, so Mark doesn't grade it like a science book. Mark sorts its claims into three kinds: supported by research, to be read with care, and matters of faith. Tap each row for the reason.

3 SOLID
3 CAUTION
2 FAITH
SOLIDMindful breathing and meditation reduce anxiety

Meta-analyses of mindfulness programmes (such as Goyal et al., 2014) show a moderate reduction in anxiety. Not a miracle, but real.

SOLIDStaying connected to the dead can help people through grief

Research on "continuing bonds" (Klass et al., 1996) finds many people are comforted by keeping loved ones in their hearts rather than having to "let go". It depends on the person and the circumstances.

SOLIDMatter is not created or destroyed, only transformed

True in physics (conservation of mass and energy). The image of cloud becoming rain is accurate as nature.

CAUTIONSince matter isn't lost, "I" am not lost either

Conservation applies to atoms and energy, not automatically to consciousness or a particular person. This is a leap from image to conclusion, and here the book persuades through feeling more than argument.

CAUTIONOriginal fear begins at the moment of birth

Nobody remembers being born. Attachment theory (Bowlby) holds something similar: infants are wholly dependent and the need for safety stays with us for life. Read it as a useful metaphor, not a real memory.

CAUTIONThinking about death helps us live more peacefully

Terror management research shows reminders of death can make people more defensive and rigid. Slow, deliberate reflection on death, though, tends to come with gratitude and clearer priorities. How you do it matters more than the content.

FAITHWe were never born and will never die

This is the Buddhist view of emptiness and interdependent co-arising. It cannot be proven or disproven by experiment. Its value lies in whether it helps the practitioner suffer less.

FAITHNirvana is available now, when notions are extinguished

One Zen reading of nirvana, unlike the idea of a realm reached after death. Thay presents it as lived experience, not theory.

Strengths
  • Simple, unforgettable images: cloud, wave, match
  • Never demands belief; invites you to look and try
  • Concrete practices for grief and for sitting with the dying
Weaknesses
  • Repetitive, since it comes from talks
  • Metaphor sometimes stands in for argument
  • Sidesteps the hard question: does "my" consciousness go on?

Read slowly, read when you need it. Don't read to debate; read to breathe along.

More: for readers who aren't Buddhist

Most of the book works for anyone: breathing to hold emotions, seeing loved ones in yourself, living so your "belongings" are good ones. None of this requires religious belief.

The hard part for sceptics is "there is no death". Read as "what makes me up carries on in other forms", it is very reasonable. Read as "I, with these memories and this awareness, still exist", the book does not prove it, and deliberately does not answer it head on.

The book is no substitute for therapy when fear of death or grief becomes overwhelming. It is a companion, not a doctor.

Part 6

Lessons to apply

No need to wait for a loss to start practising. An ordinary day, following the book:

Rename your birthdayCall it a "continuation day" and remember all that made you.
Live for your belongingsAsk each night: what words and deeds did I leave today?
Say loving words while you canDon't wait for the goodbye.
"Birth and death are only doors through which we pass. Birth and death are a game of hide-and-seek."From the gatha "This body is not me", near the end of the book.
