Part 1

Core thesis

A super memory is not magic. It is a set of techniques anyone can learn: turn information into images, link the images together, then place them somewhere familiar.

Part 4

How solid is the evidence

The book's main claims checked against memory research. Tap a row for the reason.

5 SOLID
3 CAUTION
2 WRONG
SOLIDThe memory palace greatly boosts ordinary people's recall

Dresler et al. (Neuron, 2017): novices trained 30 minutes a day for 6 weeks; words recalled rose from about 26 to 62 out of 72, and most of the gain remained after 4 months.

SOLIDMemory champions don't have unusual brains

Maguire et al. (2003) scanned 10 superior memorisers: normal intelligence and brain structure. The difference was a spatial strategy; 9 of 10 used the memory palace.

SOLIDSpaced review beats cramming for retention

Meta-analysis of over 300 experiments (Cepeda et al., 2006). Dunlosky et al. (2013) rate spaced practice and self-testing as "high utility".

SOLIDForgetting is a normal function of the brain

Richards and Frankland (Neuron, 2017): forgetting drops needless detail and helps generalisation. Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve was replicated in 2015.

SOLIDThe brain has a dedicated "map" for places

Place cells and grid cells in and around the hippocampus, Nobel Prize in Medicine 2014 (O'Keefe, May-Britt and Edvard Moser).

CAUTIONThe more bizarre the image, the better

The effect is real when odd images are mixed with ordinary ones, but fades when everything is odd (McDaniel and Einstein, 1986). Interaction between images is the more reliable factor.

CAUTIONLearning vocabulary with image keywords

Strong right after learning (Atkinson and Raugh, 1975), but some studies find faster forgetting than rote after a week without review (Wang and Thomas, 1995). Dunlosky (2013) rates it "low utility" for learning in general.

CAUTIONThree months of training gets you 1,000 digits

That is the author's own result, with a coach. Research shows everyone improves, but at very different speeds. Preparing for a world championship usually takes hours a day for months.

WRONGShort-term memory lasts from seconds to weeks

According to one review, that is the book's definition. Psychology counts short-term memory as seconds up to about 30 seconds, holding roughly 4 to 7 items. Anything still there after weeks is long-term memory.

MISREADTraining digit memory improves memory in general

The book doesn't promise this, but readers easily assume it. The skill is narrow: a man trained to recall 80 digits (Ericsson and Chase, 1980) still recalled only about 6 letters. Memory athletes lose their keys like everyone else.

Read chapter 2 and do the exercises. Use the techniques for things you must recall word for word, not as a substitute for understanding.

More: is a super memory worth the training?

One reviewer compared it with Joshua Foer's Moonwalking with Einstein and found it thinner on science, simplified to the point of inaccuracy in places. It also skips episodic and procedural memory, both directly relevant to the memory palace and to learning.

Joshua Foer, 2006 US Memory Champion, wrote that after the contest he hardly used the techniques, since paper and phones exist. They pay off in narrow places: vocabulary, formulas, speeches, names.

For students, the biggest gain probably comes from what the book says less about: self-testing and spaced review. Both work for every subject, including the parts you need to understand.
