Part 1

The question of the case

An exchange that raised more than $1.8 billion from investors, why did it collapse in a few days? Not because crypto prices fell, but because customer money was not where customers thought it was, and nobody independent was in a position to notice.

Source: SEC press release 2022-219 (more than $1.8 billion from equity investors, since May 2019).

Part 2

What happened

Up for three years, down in about nine days. Dates come from court and regulator records; the November 2022 dates before the filing are secondary sources.

From May 2019 · FundraisingMore than $1.8 billion from equity investors, about $1.1 billion from about 90 U.S. investors. They bought believing FTX had proper risk controls.
2019–2022 · Two firms, one ledgerCustomer fiat went into Alameda's bank accounts. Alameda staff credited FTX's internal ledger by hand.
2 Nov 2022 · The leakCoinDesk published Alameda's leaked balance sheet, heavy in FTT, a token issued by FTX itself (secondary source).
6–9 Nov 2022 · The runBinance said it would sell FTT, signed a letter of intent to buy FTX, then walked away after looking at the books (secondary source).
11 Nov 2022 · Chapter 11FTX and Alameda filed in Delaware. Bankman-Fried resigned. John J. Ray III became CEO.
17 Nov 2022 · Ray's declaration"Never in my career have I seen such a complete failure of corporate controls."
13 Dec 2022 · Three cases in one dayThe SEC and CFTC sued; the criminal indictment was unsealed in New York.
2 Nov 2023 and 28 Mar 2024 · SDNYA jury convicted on 7 counts. Sentence: 25 years in prison, $11 billion forfeiture.
Oct 2024 · Repayment planThe bankruptcy court confirmed it: 98% of creditors get about 119% of allowed claims, valued at filing-date prices.
12 Jun 2026 · AppealThe Second Circuit unanimously affirmed the conviction and sentence.

Source: SEC press release 2022-219; CFTC complaints (13 and 21 Dec 2022); Ray declaration (17 Nov 2022, via CNBC); DOJ sentencing release (28 Mar 2024); Chapter 11 plan confirmed Oct 2024 (via Finance Magnates, secondary source); US v. Bankman-Fried, No. 24-961 (2d Cir., 12 Jun 2026).

Part 3

Why: four layers of cause

Structure: the money holder kept the books

no segregation of dutiesCFTC, Dec 2022

Code: one account above the common rules

allow negative · no auto-liquidationSEC, CFTC

Organisation: no trustworthy books

no controlsRay, 17 Nov 2022 and 9 Apr 2023

Spending approved by emoji

Payment requests went through a chat platform; a disparate group of supervisors approved them with personalised emojis (Ray declaration, 17 Nov 2022).

No idea how much cash

No centralised control of cash, no accurate list of bank accounts and signatories (Ray declaration).

Company money, personal names

Corporate funds bought homes in the Bahamas; some property was recorded in the names of employees and advisers (Ray declaration, via CNBC).
Never in my career have I seen such a complete failure of corporate controls and such a complete absence of trustworthy financial information as occurred here.John J. Ray III, declaration to the Delaware bankruptcy court, 17 Nov 2022. Ray had led Enron after its bankruptcy.

Further source: Ray told the U.S. House on 13 Dec 2022 that FTX used QuickBooks and passed invoices around by chat (secondary source: Fortune); the First Interim Report (9 Apr 2023) found missing controls in four areas: governance, finance and accounting, digital asset management, cybersecurity.

Motive: trust in place of checking

trust as a substitute for verificationSEC; Ray, 26 Jun 2023
Part 4

Fact check: what is true and what is myth

12 things often said about FTX, checked against bankruptcy filings, complaints and the judgment. Tap a row to see why.

5 SOLID
3 CAUTION
4 FALSE
SOLIDPayments were approved by emoji

Ray declaration (17 Nov 2022): payment requests went through a chat platform and "a disparate group of supervisors" approved them with personalised emojis. The Third Circuit (2024) quotes this passage.

SOLIDFTX ran its money on QuickBooks

Ray told the U.S. House on 13 Dec 2022 (Fortune, secondary source for the wording). The problem was not the software but the absence of an accounting process around it.

SOLIDAlameda had a near-unlimited credit line on FTX

The SEC and CFTC both say "virtually unlimited". Gary Wang testified to $65 billion at trial (secondary source). Accounts of the starting limit differ, so this piece does not use one.

SOLID"The man who cleaned up Enron said he had never seen worse"

True in substance: Ray wrote "never in my career", and he led Enron after its bankruptcy. But "worse than Enron" is a headline, not his wording.

SOLIDBankman-Fried got 25 years and lost his appeal

Convicted on 7 counts (2 Nov 2023); 25 years and $11 billion forfeiture (28 Mar 2024). The Second Circuit affirmed unanimously on 12 Jun 2026, calling the evidence "robust".

CAUTION"Customers got over 100%, so nobody lost"

The plan pays about 119% to 98% of creditors, but claims are in dollars at 11 Nov 2022 prices, not later crypto prices. Many creditors objected to that, and they waited more than two years (secondary source: The Block, NBC). An early-2024 estimate was 118%.

CAUTION"Bankman-Fried acted alone and nobody knew"

The court found him the one who directed it. But the CFTC also sued Caroline Ellison and Gary Wang; they pleaded guilty and testified at trial. Wang said he wrote the code on request.

Source: CFTC amended complaint, 21 Dec 2022; US Attorney's Office SDNY press release, 21 Dec 2022, on the guilty pleas of Ellison and Wang; Wang's trial testimony (secondary source: press coverage of the October 2023 trial).

CAUTION"Binance deliberately killed FTX"

Binance's public sale of FTT lit the run (secondary source). But an exchange holding all customer money can pay a run. Binance's motive was not what the court examined in this case.

FALSE"It was just a crypto crash"

Customer money went into Alameda accounts and was used for Alameda (CFTC); $8.7 billion of customer assets was misused (Ray's report, Jun 2023). The jury convicted for fraud, not for "losing on trades".

FALSE"FTX lost the money in a hack"

There was a real unauthorised drain, but on the night of 11–12 Nov 2022, after the bankruptcy filing. Estimates of the amount conflict (secondary source: Elliptic, Halborn). The hole in customer assets came first.

FALSE"Customers lost everything"

The plan confirmed in Oct 2024: 98% of creditors receive about 119% of allowed claims; distributable assets estimated at $14.7 to $16.5 billion (secondary source). See the Caution row above on how it is valued.

FALSE"Big-name investors checked it, so FTX had to be fine"

SEC: investors bought shares believing FTX had proper risk controls, while Alameda's privileges were hidden. A famous investor's name does not replace the question "who holds the money".

The market did not kill FTX. One group held the money, kept the books and wrote the rules, and nobody stood in between to ask again.

More: why this looks more like an old bank failure than a tech story

Take out the word "crypto" and what remains is a very old story: customer deposits lent to a related party without matching collateral, exposed only when customers come for their money at once.

Ray himself wrote that the root causes were familiar: "hubris, incompetence, and greed". What was new was the speed, and that every privilege sat in a few lines of code only a small group could read.

Source for "More": Ray's First Interim Report (9 Apr 2023), the phrase "hubris, incompetence, and greed"; the rest is Mark's commentary.

Part 6

Work lesson: separate the duties

No one both holds the money and keeps the books. Applies to a team fund, an ad budget, inventory, admin rights, even shared passwords.

Two people for every sum of moneyWhoever spends does not record. Whoever records does not hold the card or the bank password.
Reconcile against the real thingEach month, compare the books with the bank statement or the actual stock. The reconciler is not in the spending group.
Exceptions must be visibleEvery "special account" or over-limit right goes on a list the reconciler can read.
Approvals leave a trailAn emoji in a chat is not an approval. An approval has a person, an amount, a reason and a date.
Check your own team: 3 questions, 10 minutes

1. List the 3 most important things your team manages: a fund, an ad budget, stock, admin accounts.

2. For each, write names: who holds it, who records it, who reconciles it. If two boxes share a name, that is where to separate.

3. Ask: is there an exception only one person knows about? If so, write it down and hand it to the reconciler.
