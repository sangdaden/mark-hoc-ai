Essence

One operation, 1953To treat severe epilepsy, a surgeon removed the inner temporal lobe on both sides of H.M.'s brain, including most of the hippocampus.
New events would not stayHis memory quotient fell to 67, where 100 is average. Whatever just happened slipped away once he was distracted.
Intelligence intactIQ 112 in 1955, higher than 104 before surgery. Remembering is not the same as thinking.
Skills still learnedTracing a star in a mirror, he improved over three days without remembering any practice.
Memory comes in kindsRemembering events needs the hippocampus. Remembering how to do things does not. That changes how we study.

Source: Scoville and Milner (1957) (surgery, IQ 104 and 112, memory quotient 67); Milner (1962), via the reviews of Squire (2009) and Corkin (2002) (mirror drawing).

Part 1

The paper's question

Where in the brain does memory live? Before 1957 many thought it was spread across the whole cortex. One operation that went tragically wrong pointed to a specific place, and showed that "remembering" is not one job but several.

The question and its framing are Mark's, based on the review by Squire (2009), Neuron 61: 6–9.

Part 2

The story of the discovery

Who, where, how, against the medicine of the 1950s.

1926 · Henry Molaison is bornIn Connecticut, USA. Minor seizures began around age 10, major ones from 16. The drugs of the day could not stop them.
1953 · Surgery in HartfordNeurosurgeon William Scoville removed the inner temporal lobe on both sides, including most of the hippocampus and the amygdala. Henry was 27. Scoville himself called it a "frankly experimental" operation.
After surgery · Something no one expectedThe seizures eased. But H.M. could not find the way to the hospital bathroom, did not recognise people he had just met, and reread old magazines as if new.
April 1955 · Brenda Milner comes to testA psychologist from the Montreal Neurological Institute (Canada). IQ 112, higher than 104 before surgery. Memory quotient 67. He still thought the year was 1953.
1957 · The paperScoville and Milner compared H.M. with 9 other people given similar operations for mental illness. Everyone whose hippocampus was removed on both sides lost memory; the more removed, the worse.
1962 · The star in the mirrorMilner had H.M. trace the outline of a star while watching only its mirror image. Over three days he got better and better, each time saying he had never done it.
2008 · His real name is releasedHenry Molaison died on 2 December 2008, aged 82. Nearly 100 researchers had worked with him over more than 50 years.

Source: Scoville and Milner (1957) (surgery, 10 patients, IQ, memory quotient, April 1955; the "still thought it was 1953" detail per reviews of the case); Dossani et al. (2015), World Neurosurgery ("frankly experimental"); Milner (1962) via Squire (2009); Corkin (2002), Nature Reviews Neuroscience 3: 153–160 (nearly 100 researchers); birth year, onset ages and date of death per Wikipedia and Science Friday (secondary source). The bathroom and magazine details are as described in reviews of the case (secondary source).

Context in the 1950s: why such an operation?

It was the era of brain surgery for mental illness (such as frontal lobotomy). Scoville had operated on the inner temporal lobe of many patients with schizophrenia, with mixed results. For H.M., surgery seemed worth trying because epilepsy made a normal life almost impossible.

No one yet knew the hippocampus was needed for memory. The 1957 paper itself warned surgeons: do not remove the hippocampus on both sides.

Source: summary of the 2000 reprint in the Journal of Neuropsychiatry and Clinical Neurosciences 12(1); Dossani et al. (2015).

Part 3

What the paper says, in 3 sentences

1. Removing the hippocampus and nearby tissue on both sides of the brain takes away the ability to lay down lasting memories of new events.

2. Intelligence, language, immediate recall and most old memories remain.

3. Removing one side, or less tissue, causes little or no memory loss: severity follows the extent of removal.

Part 5

Fact check: what is true and what is myth

14 things often said about H.M., checked against the original paper and later studies. Tap a row to see why.

6 SOLID
5 CAUTION
3 FALSE
SOLIDAfter the 1953 surgery H.M. could barely form new memories of events

Memory quotient 67 in 1955; he did not recognise people met many times. Studies over the next 50 years agreed (Corkin 2002).

SOLIDHis intelligence did not decline

IQ 112 in April 1955 versus 104 in August 1953 before surgery (Scoville and Milner 1957).

SOLIDMemory loss tracked how much hippocampus was removed on both sides

Of the paper's 10 patients, only those with damage to the hippocampus and hippocampal gyrus on both sides lost memory; more removal, worse loss.

SOLIDH.M. still learned new skills without remembering the practice

Mirror-tracing a star, improving over three days (Milner 1962, via Squire 2009).

SOLIDImmediate recall (a few digits, a short sentence) was normal

The paper reports intact immediate memory; the loss showed after distraction or a few minutes (Squire 2009).

SOLIDThe surgeon himself called it experimental

Scoville used the phrase "frankly experimental" (quoted in Dossani et al. 2015).

CAUTION"The surgery removed 8 cm"

That was Scoville's estimate. MRI in 1997 measured about 5.4 cm and 5.1 cm.

CAUTION"The hippocampus stores all our memories"

The hippocampus is needed to record and consolidate new memories of events. H.M. still remembered his childhood, so old memories are not all stored there (Squire and Wixted 2011). Spens and Burgess (2024) model the hippocampus as a fast recorder that later "teaches" the cortex.

CAUTION"All of H.M.'s deficits came from losing the hippocampus"

The 2014 brain study found an extra small lesion in the left frontal cortex and white-matter damage (Annese et al. 2014).

CAUTION"His epilepsy was caused by a bicycle accident"

Often told, but a mild injury at age 7 is unlikely to be the cause; three family members had epilepsy (Tidsskriftet, Journal of the Norwegian Medical Association, 2026; secondary source).

CAUTION"A 2016 book proved misconduct in the H.M. research"

Those are Luke Dittrich's allegations. MIT rejected them and over 200 scientists signed a letter supporting Corkin. No official finding (STAT, Undark; secondary source).

FALSE"H.M. could never learn anything new"

He learned skills (Milner 1962) and even a little new knowledge: cued with first names, he gave the right surnames for 12 of 35 people who became famous after 1953 (O'Kane, Kensinger and Corkin 2004, Hippocampus 14: 417–425).

FALSE"His entire hippocampus was removed"

About 2 cm at the back remained, shrunken (Corkin et al. 1997; Annese et al. 2014).

FALSE"H.M. forgot his whole past"

He remembered his childhood; what he lost was mostly a short period before the surgery (Scoville and Milner 1957; Milner 1966, secondary source).

Source: as noted in each row; full list in Part 10.

Part 6

What the latest research says

From the 1957 paper to a computer model in 2024.

1997 · MRI scanThe removal was shorter than estimated; about 2 cm of posterior hippocampus remained but was not enough for normal memory.
2004 · He still learned a little knowledgeCued with first names, he gave the right surnames for 12/35 people famous after 1953, and picked famous names from fake ones about 87% of the time. Learning, but very slow and little.
2011 · Review "since H.M."Squire and Wixted sum up: there are several memory systems; the hippocampus and nearby areas are needed for events and facts, not for skills.
2014 · H.M.'s brain cut into 2,401 slicesJacopo Annese's team (San Diego) rebuilt the brain in 3D: hippocampal tissue remained, plus a small lesion in the left frontal cortex. The data are open to everyone.
2024 · The hippocampus as a "fast note-taker"Spens and Burgess (UCL) built a model: the hippocampus records each event quickly, then replays it so the cortex gradually learns general knowledge. It is a model, not direct evidence.

Source: Corkin et al. (1997), J Neurosci 17(10); O'Kane, Kensinger and Corkin (2004), Hippocampus 14(4): 417–425; Squire and Wixted (2011), Annu Rev Neurosci 34: 259–288; Annese et al. (2014), Nature Communications 5: 3122 (2,401 slices, cut in December 2009); Spens and Burgess (2024), Nature Human Behaviour, doi 10.1038/s41562-023-01799-z. No new 2023–2025 study on H.M.'s own data was found (searched 15 Oct 2026).

One man's tragedy gave a whole field its map: remembering events and remembering how travel by two different roads.

More: why does one case carry so much weight?

Usually a single case is weak evidence. H.M. is different for three reasons: exactly which areas were removed is known (and was later remeasured), there were scores from before surgery to compare, and he lived another 55 years, so many experiments could be run and rerun.

The paper was also not only about H.M.: 9 other people showed the same pattern. That is why the label is Solid, even though the 8 cm figure has been corrected.

Part 8

Lessons: "knowing" and "being able to do" are two different kinds of learning

Your brain has at least two roads to memory. Send each thing you learn down the right one.

Two columnsWrite down what this subject asks you to "know" (concepts, formulas) and to "do" (solve, write, pronounce).
Skills: do it, in small dosesReading a worked solution does not replace solving. A little each day, many days, like H.M. and his star.
Knowledge: close the book, retellEvent memory has to be recorded. Retell without the book today, then again a few days later.
Distrust "it feels easy"Getting faster does not prove you understand. At each step ask "why this step?"
Self-check: the two-column test in 10 minutes

1. Pick a chapter you just studied. Draw two columns: "I can explain it" and "I can do it".

2. Left: close the book, write 3 key ideas in your own words. How many are right?

3. Right: solve a new exercise without the answer key. How far do you get?

4. Left good, right weak: you understand but have not practised. Right good, left weak: you are running on habit without knowing why.

5. Redo the test three days later. Review whichever column dropped more.

The test is Mark's suggestion, not a procedure from a study.
