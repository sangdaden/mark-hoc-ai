Part 1

Core thesis

Our intuition is usually right, but when it is wrong it is wrong in predictable ways. Knowing the names of those errors helps us notice when to slow down.

Part 4

How solid is the evidence?

The 13 most-quoted claims, rechecked after psychology's replication crisis. Tap a row to see why.

5 SOLID
4 CAUTION
4 WRONG
SOLIDAnchors bias our numeric estimates

Replicated in the Many Labs project (2014) with thousands of people across many countries.

SOLIDFraming as "gain" or "loss" changes choices

The "Asian disease" problem replicates reliably, including in Many Labs. The effect shrinks when people deliberate.

SOLIDThe conjunction fallacy (Linda)

Replicated many times. Gigerenzer counters that asking in frequencies ("how many out of 100?") sharply reduces the error.

SOLIDThe planning fallacy; use the outside view

Infrastructure data (Flyvbjerg) show cost overruns and delays are the rule, not the exception.

SOLIDSimple formulas often beat experts

From Paul Meehl (1954) to later meta-analyses, formulas win or tie in most comparisons.

CAUTIONSystem 1 and System 2 are two real systems

Kahneman himself calls them "useful fictions". Many researchers see thinking as a continuum rather than a clean split.

CAUTIONLosses hurt about twice as much as gains

Loss aversion is real and meta-analyses support it. Its size varies with context; for small stakes it can be very weak (Gal and Rucker, 2018).

CAUTIONAbove ~$75,000 a year, income no longer improves daily mood

In 2023 Kahneman and Killingsworth reanalyzed together: the plateau holds only for the least happy group; most people keep feeling better as income rises.

CAUTIONThe "hot hand" in basketball is an illusion

Miller and Sanjurjo (2018) found a statistical flaw in the original study; corrected, a small effect appears.

WRONGPriming: reading words about old age makes you walk slower

Failed to replicate (Doyen et al., 2012). In 2017 Kahneman wrote that he had "placed too much faith in underpowered studies" in chapter 4.

WRONGWillpower is a depletable resource, restored by glucose

A preregistered study across 23 labs with over 2,000 people (Hagger et al., 2016) found an effect near zero.

WRONGHard-to-read fonts make us think more carefully

A meta-analysis of 17 experiments (Meyer et al., 2015) found no effect.

CRITICIZEDHungry judges grant less parole before meals

The drop is too large to be hunger. Case order was not random: cases without a lawyer tended to come last in a session.

Keep Parts II to V. Read chapters 3–4 as history of science, not as conclusions.

More: the book and the replication crisis

The book came out in 2011, just before social psychology found that many famous results did not replicate. In 2012 Kahneman himself wrote an open letter warning of a "train wreck looming" in priming research.

In 2017 Schimmack, Heene and Kesavan rated the replicability of the studies cited in chapter 4 and found it very low. Kahneman replied publicly that he had placed too much faith in underpowered studies, and that readers should discount the chapter.

Gerd Gigerenzer criticizes from another angle: System 1's shortcuts are often smart strategies in real environments, not just errors. Kahneman also admitted that knowing about biases barely helped him avoid them; organizational procedures work better.

Lessons to apply

Five steps before a big decision. Leave small everyday choices to System 1.

Name your number before you hear theirsPrices, salaries: have your own figure first so you don't get anchored.
Look at the whole series, not each betTake many small favorable risks; judge them together, not one at a time.
Ask "what am I missing?"The smoother the story, the more you should ask.
"Nothing in life is as important as you think it is, while you are thinking about it."The focusing illusion, chapter 38.
