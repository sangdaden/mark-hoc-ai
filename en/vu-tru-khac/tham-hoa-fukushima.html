Essence

A wave higher than the wallThe plant was designed for a tsunami of at most 5.7 m. The real one was about 13–15 m.
The 15.7 m figure from 2008TEPCO itself calculated this wave height three years earlier, then sent it off for "further study".
The regulated held the penThe Diet commission called it "regulatory capture": the operator led the agency meant to supervise it.
Three kinds of deathThe tsunami killed nearly 16,000 people. Evacuation and displacement brought over 2,000 more. Acute radiation sickness: 0.
"Simply unthinkable"IAEA: the whole system believed an accident this size could not happen, so it did not prepare.

Sources: NAIIC (2012), pp. 16 and 20; TEPCO (24 Apr 2012, the 15.7 m calculation); TEPCO (8 Jul 2011, wave about 13 m); World Nuclear Association (wave about 15 m); Japan's National Police Agency (Mar 2026); Reconstruction Agency (data to 31 Dec 2025); IAEA, Director General's report (2015).

Part 1

The question of the case

A major power company had calculated, three years in advance, a wave high enough to swamp its plant. Why did that number stay in a drawer? Because an expensive warning is very easy to call "just one estimate".

→

Sources: TEPCO (24 Apr 2012); World Nuclear Association (timeline, based on NAIIC).

Part 2

What happened

One chart tells most of the story: the wave had been calculated, but the wall was never raised.

11 Mar 2011, 14:46 · M9.0 earthquakeThe three operating reactors (1, 2, 3) shut down automatically. But a shut-down reactor still makes heat and must still be cooled.
About 15:42 · Total blackoutThe tsunami overtopped the site. Almost an hour after the quake the plant lost power: 12 of 13 backup generators failed, and switchgear and batteries flooded.
12 Mar, 15:36 · Explosion, Unit 1 buildingHydrogen produced by overheated fuel leaked into the building, mixed with air and ignited. Not a nuclear explosion.
14 Mar, about 11:00 · Explosion, Unit 3 buildingAn even larger hydrogen explosion.
15 Mar, about 6:00 · Explosion, Unit 4 buildingUnit 4 was not operating at the time of the quake, yet the explosion blew apart the top of the building.
About a month later · Level 7The top of the international INES scale, the same level as Chernobyl. Japan first rated it level 5.

Sources: World Nuclear Association, "Fukushima Daiichi Accident" (quake time, blackout, 12/13 generators, explosions, INES rating); NAIIC (2012), p. 16. WNA is an industry secondary source; these milestones match the IAEA report.

Part 3

Why: five layers of cause

The terrain layer: the plant was lowered towards the sea

site grade · 10 mIAEA, Technical Volume 2, pp. 5–7

The warning layer: the 15.7 m figure stayed in a drawer

ignored estimateTEPCO 2012; NAIIC pp. 16, 27

The oversight layer: the supervised led the supervisor

regulatory captureNAIIC pp. 9, 20

The belief layer: "simply unthinkable"

safety mythIAEA 2015, foreword

Believing it was safe

Diet commission: they "had firmly committed themselves to the idea that nuclear power plants were safe", so preparing for a severe accident looked unnecessary.

An expensive warning

Accepting 15.7 m meant a seawall, flood-proofing and permits. The defendants later called the figure "nothing more than one estimate".

Delay instead of refusal

Nobody said "no". The warning went off for more study and deadlines slipped, until the wave arrived first.

Sources: NAIIC (2012), pp. 17 and 27; "nothing more than one estimate": the defence at the criminal trial, per Asahi (Apr 2018, secondary).

The response layer: once the power went, every script expired

station blackoutNAIIC pp. 16–18

No script for a long blackout

The commission wrote that, given the deficiencies in training and preparation, once the total blackout including DC power occurred, "it was impossible to change the course of events".

A tangled chain of command

The Prime Minister went to the site in person to direct workers. The commission called it a direct intervention that confused the chain of command.

Residents told too late

The commission traced the confusion of the evacuation to the regulators' negligence in emergency preparation.

Sources: NAIIC (2012), pp. 16–19; the blackout quotation via World Nuclear News (5 Jul 2012).

Part 4

Fact check: what is true and what is myth

13 things often said about Fukushima, checked against the primary reports. Click each line for the reasoning.

4 SOLID
6 CAUTION
3 FALSE
SOLIDA tsunami above the design basis caused the total blackout

Design basis 5.7 m, site grade 10 m, wave about 13–15 m. IAEA: the tsunami levels far exceeded the hazard assumptions. Diet commission: the total blackout was the point of no return.

SOLIDTEPCO had a 15.7 m tsunami estimate in 2008

TEPCO published the calculation itself (24 Apr 2012), with the defence that the wave-source model was "in question". Court testimony says management decided not to adopt it in July 2008.

SOLIDNobody died of acute radiation sickness

IAEA (2015): no early radiation-induced health effects were observed among workers or the public. Six workers received over 250 mSv, but below levels that cause radiation sickness (WNA).

SOLIDThe evacuation itself killed people

Diet commission: 60 patients from hospitals and nursing homes in the 20 km zone died in March from complications related to the evacuation. Fukushima Prefecture had 2,350 disaster-related deaths by the end of 2025 (Reconstruction Agency), though that figure does not separate out the nuclear cause.

CAUTION"A disaster Made in Japan", caused by Japanese culture

These are the words of the commission's own chairman, Kiyoshi Kurokawa, in the English edition: the root causes were "our reflexive obedience; our reluctance to question authority … our groupism; and our insularity". But according to critics cited by AP, the passage is missing from the Japanese text, and the English edition itself says the Japanese version prevails where they differ. Professor Gerald Curtis (Columbia University) wrote: "To pin the blame on culture is the ultimate cop-out." The IAEA wrote that the contributing factors "were not unique to Japan". And Onagawa, same culture, survived. The body of the report names specific, fixable failures: a captured regulator, slipped deadlines, no preparation for a blackout.

CAUTION"Nobody died from Fukushima radiation"

Nobody died of acute radiation sickness. But in 2018 Japan's health ministry accepted that a worker's lung cancer (about 195 mSv cumulative) was caused by radiation, and his family was to be compensated (NPR). UNSCEAR says any increase in radiation-related cancer is "unlikely to be discernible", meaning too small to measure, which is not the same as certainly zero.

CAUTION"Nobody could have foreseen that wave"

The official sources disagree. Diet commission: the direct causes "were all foreseeable". Tokyo District Court, civil case (2022): the accident "could have been avoided" by making the main buildings watertight. But the criminal courts (2019, 2023, Supreme Court 2025) acquitted three executives, and the Tokyo High Court (June 2025) overturned the civil ruling, finding long-term forecasts were not data that demanded immediate action.

CAUTION"Only the tsunami; the earthquake did no damage"

The Diet commission said Unit 1 may have been damaged by the quake, possibly a small loss of coolant, before the wave hit. According to WNA, the Nuclear Regulation Authority concluded in 2013 that the quake did not damage Unit 1. Unresolved.

CAUTION"Child thyroid cancer soared because of radiation"

Detected cases did rise, but UNSCEAR (2021) attributes that to "ultrasensitive screening procedures" applied to all children, and says radiation-induced thyroid cancer is "not likely to be discernible, in any of the age groups considered, including to children".

CAUTION"Fukushima was as bad as Chernobyl"

Same level 7 on the INES scale. But the estimated release was about 10–15% of Chernobyl's (Japanese agency estimates from 2011, per WNA), and UNSCEAR (2013) found the health risks "far lower than those for Chernobyl".

FALSE"Fukushima radiation killed thousands, tens of thousands"

This confuses the earthquake and tsunami deaths (15,901, National Police Agency) with the nuclear accident. UNSCEAR: no adverse health effects among Fukushima residents have been documented that are directly attributable to radiation exposure.

FALSE"The reactors exploded like a nuclear bomb"

The explosions in buildings 1, 3 and 4 were hydrogen explosions: hydrogen produced by overheated fuel cladding leaked into the buildings, mixed with air and ignited. They blew off roofs and walls; there was no nuclear chain-reaction explosion.

FALSE"The treated-water release poisoned the Pacific"

IAEA (comprehensive report, 4 Jul 2023): TEPCO's approach is "consistent with relevant international safety standards" and the discharges "would have a negligible radiological impact on people and the environment". Concerns about trust and fisheries are real, but "poisoning the ocean" has no measured basis.

Sources: NAIIC (2012), pp. 9, 16–17, 38; IAEA (2015); TEPCO (2012); UNSCEAR, press release of 9 Mar 2021 on the 2020/2021 report; AP (13 Sep 2012) on the "Made in Japan" debate; AFP (Mar 2025) and AP (Jun 2025) on the rulings; NPR (5 Sep 2018); IAEA on treated water (4 Jul 2023), as quoted by Japan's Ministry of the Environment.

Assessing the case

Done right after the disaster
  • The Diet set up its first independent investigation commission, with public hearings
  • A new nuclear regulator, separated from promotion (2012)
  • IAEA and UNSCEAR published international assessments, updated over years
Not done right
  • No executive convicted; the courts disagree on responsibility
  • About 27,000 people still unable to return home (Nov 2025, per WNA)
  • The "culture" explanation blurred the specific failures

Not simply a natural disaster: nature brought the wave, but people decided how high the wall would be.

More: why "avoidable" scores 4/5 and not 5/5

An M9 quake and a 13–15 m wave are genuinely extreme. Nobody can say for sure whether flood-proofing begun in 2008 would have been finished before March 2011. The 2022 civil court found that making the main buildings watertight would have taken about two years and been enough to avoid the accident; the High Court in 2025 disagreed that the executives were obliged to act immediately.

But Onagawa shows that better preparation was possible on the very same coast. So Mark gives 4: largely avoidable, with some uncertainty.

Sources: Tokyo District Court civil ruling, 13 Jul 2022 (Asahi, secondary); Tokyo High Court, 6 Jun 2025 (AP); 27,000 people: World Nuclear Association.

Part 6

Work lessons: don't dismiss a warning because it is expensive

Use this when someone brings a risk that would cost money, time or schedule to address: security, backups, safety, legal.

Compare with the worst credible scenarioThe 5.7 m design was based on one known wave. The right question is "what is the worst case the experts have already written down?"
Backups must not sit with the riskThe backup generators flooded with the same wave. At work: backups on the same server, a plan B that depends on the same person.
Find an "Onagawa" to compare withHow do competitors, the team next door or another industry handle the same risk? The difference is evidence, not "different circumstances".
Count the cost of the response tooA rushed evacuation also kills. Emergency plans must be rehearsed with the most vulnerable people, not only on paper.
Continuous questioning and openness to learning from experience are key to safety culture.Yukiya Amano, IAEA Director General, foreword to the report on Fukushima Daiichi (2015), via World Nuclear News.
