Does AI help or harm learning

Bastani et al. · "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics" · PNAS 2025

PaperPNAS vol. 122, no. 26, article e2422633122 (2025)
Experimentnearly 1,000 high school students in Turkey, randomly split into 3 groups
Findingwith a chatbot practice improved; without it, exams were 17% worse
LabelCaution: well designed, open data, but new and not yet replicated

Source: Bastani et al. (2025), PNAS 122(26), e2422633122, abstract; the authors' public data (github.com/obastani/GenAICanHarmLearning). The label is Mark's assessment.

One-minute recap

Essence

Three groups, one schoolNo AI, a plain chatbot, and a chatbot with "guardrails" that only gives hints.
Practice: way upWith AI, practice scores rose 48% (plain chatbot) and 127% (hint chatbot).
Exam without AI: downThe plain-chatbot group scored 17% lower than students who never had it. AI became a "crutch".
Hints instead of answersA chatbot giving only teacher-written hints removed most of the harm, though exam scores did not rise either.
Design decidesAt Harvard, a carefully built AI tutor helped students learn more than an active-learning class.

Source: Bastani et al. (2025), PNAS abstract (48%, 127%, 17%, teacher-designed hints); Mark reran the main analysis on the public data and got the same numbers; Kestin et al. (2025), Scientific Reports 15, 17458, abstract.

Part 2

The story of the discovery

Who did it, where, why, and what was known before.

1984 · The dream of a personal tutorBloom wrote about the "2 sigma problem": one-to-one tutoring far outperforms a large class, but nobody can afford a tutor for every student. See Paper #8.
30/11/2022 · Chatbots for everyoneChatGPT launches. Students suddenly have a free "problem solver" available at any hour.
The Bastani team's questionHamsa Bastani (Wharton, University of Pennsylvania) and colleagues partnered with a high school in Turkey to test it in real classrooms, not a lab.
DesignGrades 9, 10, 11; 4 maths review sessions. Random assignment: no AI, "GPT Base" (like plain ChatGPT), "GPT Tutor" (prompts designed to protect learning, giving teacher hints instead of answers).
7/2024 · The preprint spreadsThe paper appeared on SSRN as "Generative AI Can Harm Learning" and was widely discussed before peer review.
6/2025 · Published in PNASThe new title adds two key words: "without guardrails". The team also released the data and code.

Source: Bloom (1984), Educational Researcher; ChatGPT launch date from Wikipedia (secondary source); Bastani et al. (2025), PNAS abstract; grades and sessions from the public data (final_data.csv); Wharton per Knowledge at Wharton (secondary source); SSRN 4895486 (7/2024); online date 25/6/2025 per the CHIBE page, University of Pennsylvania (secondary source).

Part 3

What the paper says, in 3 sentences

1. With AI, practice gets much betterPractice scores rose 48% with the plain chatbot and 127% with the hint chatbot.
2. Without AI, the plain-chatbot group fallsOn the AI-free exam they scored 17% below those who never had it: they used AI as a crutch.
3. Guardrails remove most of the harmWith a chatbot giving only teacher-written hints, exam scores were about the same as the control group.

Part 5

Fact check: facts and myths

14 things often said about AI and learning. Tap each row for the reason.

6 SOLID
6 CAUTION
2 FALSE
SOLIDPublished in PNAS 2025, vol. 122, no. 26

The authors' repository README, PubMed 40560616, IDEAS/RePEc. The full title adds "Evidence from high school mathematics".

SOLIDNearly 1,000 students, grades 9–11, 4 sessions, randomised

The abstract says "nearly a thousand"; the data hold 943 students, 4 sessions, grades 9, 10, 11. The main analysis drops honors classes, leaving 839.

SOLIDWith AI, practice scores rose 48% and 127%

PNAS abstract. Mark's rerun: +48.1% and +126.9%.

SOLIDWithout AI, the plain-chatbot group scored 17% lower

PNAS abstract. Mark's rerun: −16.8% adjusted. Raw: 29% vs 32%.

SOLIDGPT-4 at the time got almost half the problems wrong

Public answer file: 290 of 570 attempts correct (51%).

SOLIDKey guardrail: teacher hints instead of answers

A sentence in the paper's abstract (ADS version).

CAUTION"The hint chatbot made students do better on exams"

It only removed the harm: exam scores were about the same as the control group (Mark estimates −1.3%, effectively no difference).

CAUTION"Plain-chatbot students did not feel they learned less"

Reported in summaries of the study (secondary source); Mark could not read this part of the original.

CAUTION"An AI tutor at Harvard beat the classroom"

Kestin et al. (2025): true for 194 students in one intro physics course, two sessions, short term (design details per press, secondary source).

CAUTION"AI tutors double learning"

Kestin's 2024 preprint said "more than twice as much". The peer-reviewed version says only "significantly more in less time".

CAUTION"MIT proved ChatGPT rots your brain"

"Your Brain on ChatGPT" (2025) is an unreviewed preprint, 54 people writing essays with EEG; a published commentary points out inconsistencies.

CAUTION"In Nigeria, 6 weeks of English with a chatbot brought clear gains"

World Bank (De Simone et al., 2025): +0.23 standard deviations in English. A working paper, not peer reviewed.

FALSE"Using AI to study always does harm"

The hint-chatbot group was not harmed; Kestin's study found benefits from a carefully built AI tutor. Harm or help depends on how it is used.

FALSE"Meta-analyses have proved ChatGPT greatly improves learning"

The best-known meta-analysis (Wang and Fan, 2025, 51 studies, g = 0.867) was retracted by the journal on 22/4/2026 over discrepancies in the analysis.

Source: as noted in each row; full list under Sources.

Part 6

What the latest research says

5/2025 · Nigeria, 6 weeksWorld Bank: first-year senior secondary students studied English with Copilot (GPT-4) for 6 weeks in a randomised trial; +0.31 standard deviations on a composite assessment, +0.23 for English. Not peer reviewed.
6/2025 · HarvardKestin et al.: an AI tutor built on teaching principles; students learned more in less time than in an active-learning class.
2025 · "An effect in search of a cause"Weidlich et al. audited experiments in a large meta-analysis: only a small minority clearly described how AI was used, what the control group did, and measured actual learning.
22/4/2026 · A meta-analysis retractedWang and Fan's pooled analysis of 51 studies, once heavily cited, was retracted for "discrepancies in the meta-analysis".

Source: De Simone et al., World Bank Policy Research Working Paper WPS11125 (19/5/2025); Kestin et al., Scientific Reports 15, 17458 (3/6/2025); Weidlich, Gašević, Drachsler, Kirschner, Journal of Computer Assisted Learning 41(5), e70105 (2025); Retraction Note, Humanities and Social Sciences Communications 13, article 528 (2026). Read only as abstracts or notice pages via search results.

In short: the right question is no longer "is AI good or bad for learning" but "how is AI designed and used". Good evidence is still scarce.

Part 8

Lessons: use AI to ask for hints, not to copy answers

For students and self-learners. No AI expertise needed.

Try alone for 10 minutes firstOpen AI only when you have tried and know where you are stuck.
Ask for a hint, not the answerSay it plainly: "Don't solve it for me. Just give me a hint for the next step."
Doubt the AI's answerIn the study, GPT-4 got almost half the problems wrong. Check each step yourself.
Test yourself without AIAt the end, close the AI and redo 2 similar problems. That is your real score.

Source: "hints instead of answers" per the abstract of Bastani et al. (2025); 290/570 correct per the public data. The habits are Mark's suggestions.

Self-check after 1 week: 3 questions

1. How many times this week did I open AI before trying myself? Target: 0.

2. Did I ask for answers or for hints? Check my last 5 chats.

3. On an AI-free test at the end of the week, is my score close to my AI-assisted homework? If it is much lower, AI has become a crutch.

If AI does the work, the homework gets better. You do not.Mark's summary of the paper's result, not a quotation.
