Essence

A fault in a haemoglobin geneThe body makes too little of one of the two chains of haemoglobin, so red cells are weak and break early.
It takes a gene from both parentsIf both parents carry it, each pregnancy has a 25% chance of an affected child.
"Rare" depends on whereIn Vietnam, 13.8% of people carry a thalassemia gene, according to a 2017 national survey.
Screening worksCyprus went from 70–80 expected cases to fewer than 5 a year, by testing carriers and counselling couples.

Part 1

Question

A disease that is "rare" in Europe sits in the genes of more than 13 percent of Vietnamese people. Why is a disease gene so common in some regions, and what did people do so that fewer children are born needing transfusions for life?

Part 2

The story

Who, where, when: from a children's clinic in the US to two Mediterranean islands and Vietnam.

1925 · Detroit, USAPaediatrician Thomas Cooley and his colleague Lee describe children of Italian and Greek descent with severe anaemia, a large spleen and changes in the facial bones. The illness becomes known by his name: "Cooley's anaemia".
1930s · A name from the seaTwo doctors in Rochester (USA), Whipple and Bradford, coin "thalassemia", from the Greek thalassa, the sea, because the first cases were all children with roots around the Mediterranean.
1949 · A question about malariaGeneticist J.B.S. Haldane asks why a disease gene is so common in Italy, Sicily and Greece. He guesses that people with one copy resist malaria better, so the gene is kept over generations.
1973 · Cyprus beginsNearly 1 in 7 Cypriots carries the gene. The island starts public education, carrier testing and counselling. From 1977 prenatal diagnosis is added. Dr Minas Angastiniotis describes the programme in The Lancet in 1981.
1983 · A paper before the weddingThe Orthodox Church in Cyprus asks couples for a certificate showing they have been tested before a church wedding. Results stay confidential, and whether to marry is still the couple's choice.
Mid-1970s · Sardinia (Italy)Dr Antonio Cao's team in Cagliari finds 12.6% of people carry the gene, then starts voluntary screening with prenatal diagnosis.
Today · VietnamA 2017 national survey: 13.8% of the population carries a thalassemia gene. The National Institute of Hematology and Blood Transfusion estimates about 8,000 babies are born with the disease each year, about 2,000 with the severe form.

In Vietnam, the rate varies widely between ethnic groups

The press, citing the National Institute of Hematology and Blood Transfusion, gives about 9.8% carriers among the Kinh majority and over 20% in some northern mountain groups (Tày 26.1%, Dao 25.5%, Nùng 24.7%). These are secondary sources, not yet checked against the original report.

The Institute team's 2022 paper also notes that rates "vary by ethnicity and topography", and that prevention relies on prenatal and newborn diagnosis.

Source: Văn hoá, Lào Cai and PLO newspapers citing the National Institute of Hematology and Blood Transfusion (secondary source); Bach et al. (2022), Hemoglobin 46(1): 62–65.

Part 3

How the gene works

1. Haemoglobin carries oxygen inside red cells and is built from four chains: two alpha and two beta. The HBB gene on chromosome 11 makes the beta chain; two genes, HBA1 and HBA2 on chromosome 16, make the alpha chain.

2. In beta-thalassemia, a faulty HBB gene makes little or no beta chain. The spare alpha chains clump together, killing young red cells in the bone marrow and making mature red cells break early: the person becomes anaemic.

3. Everyone has two copies of HBB, one from each parent. One faulty copy: a carrier, usually healthy, with slightly small red cells. Two faulty copies: the disease, and the severe form needs transfusions from 6 to 24 months of age.

Part 4

Fact check: true stories and rumours

14 things people say about thalassemia, checked against medical sources. Tap a row for the reason.

7 SOLID
4 CAUTION
3 WRONG
SOLIDA child has the severe disease only with a faulty gene from both parents

Beta-thalassemia is autosomal recessive (GeneReviews; MedlinePlus). Dominant forms are very rare.

SOLIDIf both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 25% chance of an affected child

And 50% carrier, 25% non-carrier. The odds reset with every pregnancy (GeneReviews, genetic counseling).

SOLIDCarriers are usually healthy and need no treatment

They may have very mild anaemia and small red cells, usually without symptoms (MedlinePlus Genetics).

SOLIDThe severe form shows between 6 and 24 months of age

Pallor, poor growth, mild jaundice, enlarged liver and spleen (GeneReviews, Langer 2024).

SOLIDLifelong transfusions overload the body with iron

Each transfusion adds iron the body cannot get rid of, so patients need iron chelation (GeneReviews).

SOLIDCyprus cut affected births to fewer than 5 a year

Against 70–80 expected (Telfer et al. 2006). A 2013 review gives an expected 30–50; either way the drop is very large.

SOLIDGene therapy freed most trial participants from transfusions

89% of 41 people on Zynteglo; 91% of 35 people on Casgevy with enough follow-up (FDA, via BioSpace and MLO). Single-arm trials, no decades-long data yet.

CAUTION"Vietnam has 13 million (or 14 million) carriers"

Newspapers round differently by year: 12, 13 or 14 million. The peer-reviewed figure is 13.8% of the population from the 2017 national survey (Bach et al. 2022).

CAUTION"Sardinia went from 1 in 250 to 1 in 4,000 births"

The 15-year review (about 1990) gives 1 in 1,000. The 1 in 4,000 figure is for 1996, cited in a Haematologica article; the original was not read.

CAUTION"Thalassemia genes protect against malaria"

This is Haldane's 1949 hypothesis, supported by many studies: a 2012 meta-analysis found people with two copies of mild alpha-thalassemia had about 37% lower odds of severe malaria (OR 0.63). The effect is much smaller than for the sickle gene, and the mechanism is unclear (Taylor et al. 2012, Lancet Infectious Diseases).

CAUTION"In Cyprus, carriers are banned from marrying"

Since 1983 the church has asked for a certificate of testing, not a "clean" result. Results stay confidential and couples may still marry (Cyprus Mail 2023; EJHG 2010 review table; secondary source).

WRONG"Thalassemia is contagious"

It is inherited, present in the genes from conception. It does not spread by contact, food or marriage (GeneReviews; MedlinePlus).

WRONG"Anaemia? Just take more iron"

Carriers have small red cells and are easily mistaken for iron deficiency; patients are usually iron-overloaded from transfusions. Small red cells alone cannot tell the two apart; iron studies and haemoglobin tests ordered by a doctor can (thalassemia diagnosis reference, thebloodproject.com; GeneReviews).

WRONG"Carrying the gene means being ill, and two carriers should not marry"

Carrying is not having the disease. Carrier couples can get genetic counselling and prenatal diagnosis, as Cyprus and Sardinia did (GeneReviews; Angastiniotis and Hadjiminas 1981).

Source: as given in each row; full list in Part 9.

Part 5

Where things stand

From transfusions and iron chelation to gene therapy. Each milestone has a year and a source.

Foundation · Transfusion and chelationStill the standard care for the severe form, letting children grow and develop normally. A stem cell transplant from a matched donor can cure, but not everyone has a donor.
Nov 2019 · A drug to reduce transfusionsFDA approves luspatercept. In a trial of 336 people, 21.4% cut their transfusion need by at least a third, against 4.5% on placebo.
Aug 2022 · First gene therapyFDA approves Zynteglo: the patient's own stem cells receive a working beta gene and are infused back. 89% of 41 people no longer needed transfusions.
Nov 2023 and Jan 2024 · CRISPR editingThe UK approves Casgevy first (16 Nov 2023), then the FDA (16 Jan 2024). It does not fix the beta gene; it "switches back on" fetal haemoglobin. 91% of 35 people with enough follow-up went at least 12 months in a row without a transfusion.
Dec 2025 · A pillFDA approves mitapivat for anaemia in adults with alpha- or beta-thalassemia; liver tests are required because of cases of liver injury.
Vietnam · Transplants at homeSome hospitals now perform stem cell transplants for children with thalassemia, for example Hue Central Hospital since September 2024, with 16 cases in 18 months.

Source: GeneReviews (Langer 2024) (transfusion, chelation, transplant); FDA, 8 Nov 2019, luspatercept, BELIEVE trial (via ESMO, ASCO Post); FDA, Aug 2022, Zynteglo (via MLO, BioSpace); MHRA 16 Nov 2023 and FDA 16 Jan 2024, Casgevy (via Press Herald, BioSpace); FDA 23 Dec 2025, mitapivat (via HCPLive, secondary source); Đại biểu Nhân dân newspaper 2025 on Hue Central Hospital (secondary source). Drug names are for reference only, not treatment advice.

The price of gene therapy

In the US, Casgevy is priced at about 2.2 million dollars and Zynteglo at about 2.8 million for one treatment (press, secondary source). Patients need high-dose chemotherapy to "make room" in the marrow before receiving the edited cells, and only large centres can do it.

So for most patients in the world, safe transfusions and regular chelation still decide their quality of life.

Label: SOLID. A disease "rare" in one place is a matter for millions of families in another, and the best prevention starts with a blood test.

More: why is screening a sensitive matter?

The Cyprus programme succeeded partly because the church and community stood behind it, but it also raised a question: how voluntary is it when a wedding needs a certificate? Programmes today stress counselling and the couple's own choice, and keep results confidential.

For people living with the disease, "prevention" must never turn into "should not have been born". They need good treatment, and many study, work and start families.

Part 7

Lessons: read your blood count and know what to ask

You do not need to be a doctor. You need two lines of numbers and a few questions.

For reading and understanding, not a substitute for medical advice. The cut-offs below are screening signals used in studies, not for self-diagnosis.

Keep your blood countTake a photo, note the year. If MCV and MCH stay low for years despite iron, that is worth asking about.
Test both partnersIf only one carries it, children will not have the severe form. Only when both carry it is there more to discuss.
Ask the right question"Could this result mean I carry thalassemia?", "Do I need a haemoglobin electrophoresis?"
Say "carrier", not "ill"Carriers are usually healthy. The right word reduces fear and stigma.
Self-check: five questions to take to a premarital check-up
  1. What are my MCV and MCH, and are they low?
  2. If low, should I have iron tests, haemoglobin electrophoresis, or a gene test?
  3. If I am a carrier, should my partner have the same test?
  4. If we both carry it, where can we get genetic counselling?
  5. If we have a pregnancy, when and where is prenatal diagnosis done?

The question list is Mark's suggestion, based on GeneReviews (genetic counselling, prenatal diagnosis) and Vietnam's Ministry of Health prenatal screening guidance (Decision 1807/QĐ-BYT, 2020).
