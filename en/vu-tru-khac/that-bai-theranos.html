Part 1

The question

A company raised more than $700 million, had a board full of famous names, and a big pharmacy chain as a partner. Why did nobody check the machine before believing in it? The answer is not "bad luck". A demo was treated as a product, and nobody was allowed to say "it doesn't work yet".

Source: SEC press release 2018-41 (more than $700 million); Walgreens partnership: Walgreens v. Theranos court filing (Nov 2016) as reported by the press.

Part 2

What happened

Very high on promises, very fast down once someone checked.

2003 · FoundedElizabeth Holmes, 19, leaves Stanford to start the company, first named Real-Time Cures. (secondary source)
Sept 2013 · On the shelf at WalgreensFirst blood-draw site in Palo Alto, then about 40 sites, mostly in Arizona. Real patients start receiving results. (secondary source)
2013–2015 · More than $700 millionRaised on claims about its own analyzer, military contracts and revenue. The SEC later found these claims false.
July 2015 · FDA clears one testA fingerstick test for herpes virus HSV-1. One test, not the whole system.
15 Oct 2015 · The first articleWall Street Journal: by the end of 2014 only 15 of more than 240 tests ran on the Edison device, according to a former employee. (secondary source)
25 Jan 2016 · CMS: "immediate jeopardy"After inspecting the Newark lab (Sept–Nov 2015): 5 serious violations; hematology put patients at risk.
May 2016 · Results voidedTheranos told CMS it had voided all Edison results from 2014 and 2015 and sent corrected reports to doctors. (WSJ, secondary source)
7 July 2016 · Certification revokedCMS revokes the lab's certificate and bans Holmes from owning or operating a lab for at least 2 years.
Nov 2016 · A partner turnsWalgreens sues for $140 million. Settled in 2017, amount not disclosed. (secondary source)
14 Mar 2018 · SEC: "massive fraud"Holmes settles: $500,000 penalty, returns 18.9 million shares, gives up control, 10-year bar from leading a public company.
June 2018 · Criminal indictmentHolmes and Balwani (former president and COO) charged with 2 counts of conspiracy and 9 counts of wire fraud.
Sept 2018 · Dissolution"Cash is not nearly sufficient to pay all creditors", so shareholders get nothing.
2022 · SentencesHolmes: guilty on 4 counts of defrauding investors, 135 months. Balwani: guilty on all 12 counts, 155 months. $452 million restitution. Upheld on appeal (24 Feb 2025).

Part 4

Fact check: what is true and what is myth

14 things often said about Theranos, checked against SEC, CMS and court records. Tap each row for the reason.

5 SOLID
4 CAUTION
5 FALSE
SOLIDTheranos raised more than $700 million

SEC press release 2018-41 (14 Mar 2018): Holmes, Balwani and Theranos "raised more than $700 million from investors" through false statements.

SOLID2014 revenue was projected at over $100 million; actual about $100,000

SEC complaint, via STAT and BioCentury. A MedCity piece gives the projection as $108 million; wording varies but the gap is a factor of about a thousand.

SOLIDCMS found the lab put patients in "immediate jeopardy"

CMS letter of 25 Jan 2016: 5 condition-level deficiencies; hematology was "immediate jeopardy".

SOLIDHolmes was banned from owning or operating a lab for 2 years

CMS sanctions notice of 7 July 2016, together with revocation of the Newark lab's CLIA certificate.

SOLIDTheranos voided all Edison results from 2014 and 2015

Theranos told CMS (May 2016, via WSJ); former lab director Kingshuk Das testified he voided every Edison result from those two years. The scale is disputed: see the figure above (secondary source).

CAUTION"Theranos was worth $9 billion"

That is a Forbes estimate (2014) based on a funding-round price, meaning what investors were willing to pay for a promise. On dissolution in 2018, shareholders received nothing (CEO Taylor's letter).

CAUTION"One newspaper article brought Theranos down"

The WSJ article of 15 Oct 2015 was the spark. But the binding findings came from bodies with the power to inspect: FDA (2015), CMS (2016), SEC (2018) and the courts (2022).

CAUTION"Theranos was FDA approved"

The FDA cleared only one herpes test, HSV-1 (2 July 2015). The same year the FDA recorded the "nanotainer" collection tube as an uncleared medical device (Form 483).

CAUTION"Holmes got 11 years"

Sentenced to 135 months (11 years 3 months) on 18 Nov 2022. In March 2026 the court took 1 year off under amended sentencing guidelines for first-time offenders (Bloomberg Law, secondary source). Reported release dates differ, so this article gives none.

FALSE"Every startup hypes; Theranos was just unlucky"

Optimism about the future is different from false statements about the present. The SEC found the claims about military contracts, revenue and the analyzer's capabilities false; juries convicted Holmes on 4 counts of defrauding investors and Balwani on 12. There is no "bad luck" in the record.

FALSE"One drop of blood, more than 200 tests on a Theranos machine"

SEC complaint: its own analyzer ran only about 12 of more than 200 tests on the menu. Most ran on other makers' commercial analyzers.

FALSE"Holmes was convicted of defrauding patients"

The jury acquitted Holmes on the 4 patient-related counts (3 Jan 2022). She was convicted on 4 investor-related counts. Balwani, however, was convicted on the patient counts too.

FALSE"Nobody warned anyone"

The lab's own quality-control records logged the failures (CMS-2567: 29% of Edison QC checks failed in Oct 2014). Employees reported to the New York State health department and CMS (testimony, secondary source).

FALSE"Pfizer validated Theranos's technology"

Holmes admitted at trial (Nov 2021) that she added the Pfizer and Schering-Plough logos to reports sent to Walgreens and investors; she said "I wish I had done it differently" (as reported by the press, secondary source).

What went wrong
  • Investors and partners accepted promises in place of data
  • Real patients became the test
  • SEC settled without admission; Walgreens settlement terms undisclosed

Not bad luck. A demo was sold as a product, and the people who knew the truth had no way to say "it doesn't work yet".

More: the line between "vision" and "false statement"

Every startup sells the future. The difference is the tense. "We will run 200 tests" is a risky promise. "Our machine runs 200 tests" describes the present, and can be checked.

Theranos fell over sentences in the present tense: the machine works, the military uses it, the revenue is there. The lesson for ordinary work: when you report, separate "done", "in testing" and "planned". When you hear a report, ask which tense it is in.

Source for "More": the claims come from the complaint SEC v. Holmes (14 Mar 2018); the rest is Mark's commentary.

Part 6

Work lesson: verify before you believe the demo

Works for any demo: software, AI models, prototypes, a colleague's or vendor's "it's done" report.

Watch the demo on your dataBring your own real samples, not the ones they prepared. Compare with the old method on the same samples.
Turn questions into numbers"Does it work well?" becomes "how many out of the total, and how many failures last month?"
Give someone the right to say "not yet"Name one person on the team who may stop a launch on seeing a failure, and nobody is punished for bad news.
Secrecy does not exempt from checksIf someone refuses to let an outsider look even under an NDA, treat it as a signal, not a reason.
Check your own team: 5 questions
  1. The last time someone said "this doesn't work yet", what happened to them?
  2. Did the last demo you saw run on real data or on prepared data?
  3. Has anyone outside the team checked the results by hand?
  4. How long does bad news from the front line take to reach the decision-maker?
  5. Can the number you promised your client or boss be counted today, or is it still "will"?
Optimism about the future is a founder's job. False statements about the present are not.Mark's summary of the SEC record and the US v. Holmes verdict, not a quotation.
