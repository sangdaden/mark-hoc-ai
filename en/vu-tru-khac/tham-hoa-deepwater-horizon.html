Part 1

The question of the case

The last test before temporarily abandoning the well gave a clearly abnormal result. Experienced drillers saw it, discussed it, and then chose the explanation that reassured them. Why do capable people read bad data as good data?

Part 3

Why: four barriers, all breached

An oil well is kept closed by several layers. At Macondo every layer failed, and each later layer assumed the one before it was fine.

Overview: four layers of protection

barriers · defense in depthBP 2010, key findings; CSB 2016

Layers 1–2: the negative pressure test

negative pressure testNAE, ch. 2; CSB, vol. 3

The human layer: the comforting explanation

"bladder effect" · confirmation biasNational Commission, ch. 1 and 4; NAE, ch. 2

No common criteria

This rig had used at least six different negative test procedures between August 2007 and April 2010 (CSB, vol. 3, p. 30).

Nobody outside the room looked

No one onshore or at the regulator was required to review the test data before work continued (NAE, finding 2.10).

Hindsight is easy

The CSB reminds us that behaviour is only called an error after a bad outcome. The crew was "doing what made sense to them at the time" (vol. 3, pp. 23 and 36).

Part 4

Fact check: what is true and what is myth

12 things often said about Deepwater Horizon, checked against the primary record. Click each row to see why.

5 SOLID
4 CAUTION
3 FALSE
SOLIDThe negative pressure test showed the well was not sealed, yet was judged a "pass"

Every investigation agrees. The Commission: the test "identified a cementing failure but was incorrectly judged a success because of insufficiently rigorous test procedures" (co-chairs' testimony, Jan 2011). NAE, CSB and BP's report reach the same conclusion.

SOLID11 men died

National Commission (ch. 1) and CSB (executive summary, p. 6). Seriously injured: the CSB says 17; the Commission says 16 were medevaced.

SOLIDThe well was behind schedule and over budget

Almost six weeks late and more than $58 million over a budget of $96.2 million and 51 days (National Commission, ch. 1).

SOLIDThe cement evaluation crew was sent home without running a log

On 20 April the Schlumberger team was sent home, saving time and a $128,000 fee (National Commission, ch. 1). NAE: no cement bond log was run (finding 2.9).

SOLIDThe blowout preventer had maintenance faults

CSB: the blue pod was miswired, draining a critical battery; a solenoid in the yellow pod was also miswired; the blind shear ram did not meet the manufacturer's published design (executive summary, findings 2a, 2b, 12).

CAUTION"It was only BP cutting costs"

Partly true, but incomplete. In 2014 the federal court split fault: BP 67%, Transocean 30%, Halliburton 3%, and called BP's decisions "profit-driven" (per the Christian Science Monitor). The National Commission named the root cause as "a failure of management by BP, Halliburton and Transocean", said the time-saving decisions were made "whether purposeful or not", and pointed to an under-funded regulator (MMS) under pressure to increase production. The CSB stresses human factors and how the whole industry manages safety barriers.

CAUTIONThe drill crew were careless and did not watch the well

They did miss the signs for nearly an hour. But the CSB points to poor tools: two pressure sensors on the BOP had no alarms, there was no common test procedure, and the crew was doing several things at once. The CSB says they were "doing what made sense to them at the time" (vol. 3, pp. 36 and 48).

CAUTION4.9 million barrels of oil spilled into the sea

That is the FRTG estimate (±10%), and about 800,000 barrels of it were captured. The 2015 court found 3.19 million barrels. The National Commission and the CSB say "over 4 million barrels".

CAUTIONToo few centralizers caused the blowout

It was one of the 9 risk-raising decisions the Commission lists, not the sole cause. The NAE says it is unclear whether the casing was adequately centralized, and points to the unstable foam cement as the bigger problem (ch. 2).

FALSEThe "bladder effect" is a real phenomenon that explains the 1,400 psi

The NAE calls it "an erroneous theory", and says the term as used in industry is "unrelated" to the situation in the test (ch. 2).

FALSEThe blowout preventer did nothing at all

CSB: the automatic emergency system "most likely functioned" and the blind shear ram closed, but the pipe was buckled and off-centre so it could not seal (executive summary, findings 4–5). The NAE notes that the evidence on the emergency systems is disputed.

FALSEThe rig sank right after the explosion

The rig burned for about 36 hours and sank on 22 April 2010 (National Commission, ch. 1). Oil kept flowing from the well on the seabed until 15 July.

Done right afterwards

  • The Commission named all three companies and the regulator
  • The court split fault clearly between the parties
Not done right
  • The CSB's final report took six years (2016)
  • The companies blamed each other for years
  • The Commission warned the regulator was still under production pressure

The information that could have saved 11 lives was on the gauges from 18:00. What was missing was not data, but a rule that made people believe the bad number.

More: what the Commission said about the whole industry

The Commission wrote that the blowout "could have been prevented", and that the mistakes revealed "such systematic failures in risk management that they place in doubt the safety culture of the entire industry". It called this the cost of "a culture of complacency".

On the regulator, the Commission noted that MMS's budget "decreased or remained static while deepwater oil production in the Gulf of Mexico boomed", and that safety functions needed insulating from the pressure to increase production and lease revenue.

Source: National Commission (2011), Foreword, pp. vii and ix; Recommendations, p. 8.

Part 6

Work lessons: when the data look bad, don't reach for the comforting explanation

Applies to every "final check": pre-launch testing, data reconciliation, building inspection, reviewing documents before signing.

Believe the bad number until you disprove itWhen two sources disagree, the burden of proof sits with "safe", not with "there's a problem".
Don't monitor while doing three other thingsThe Commission lists bypassing the pits and simultaneous operations among the 9 risk-raising decisions.
Turn signals into alarmsA sensor with a number but no alarm is close to no sensor. Set thresholds; let the machine prompt the person.
Don't let barriers lean on each otherEach check must stand on its own, without assuming the previous one was fine.
Human performance is often only deemed erroneous in the aftermath of a negative outcome.CSB, Macondo investigation report, vol. 3 (2016), p. 23. A reminder: the lesson is to fix the process, not just to find someone to blame.
