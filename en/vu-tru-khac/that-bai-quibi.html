Part 1

The question

Two people who had run DreamWorks Animation and eBay, $1.75 billion, a roster of Hollywood stars. Why did it close less than 7 months after launch? The familiar answer is "COVID". The answer in the record is: big money was spent before anyone knew whether real users would pay.

Source: total funding per Quibi's announcement, March 2020 (via Advanced Television, secondary source); launch 6 Apr 2020, shutdown announced 21 Oct 2020 (open letter). The founders' backgrounds are widely published public information.

Part 4

Fact check: what is true and what is myth

13 things often said about Quibi, checked against the shutdown letter, court dockets, Roku's release and the press (marked as secondary). Click each row for the reasoning.

6 CAUTION

SOLIDQuibi raised $1.75 billion before launch

Quibi announced a $750 million second round in early March 2020, for a total of $1.75 billion, about a month before launch. Many outlets agree on the figure. One mismatch: NextTV cites Whitman saying the $750 million included $400 million closed at the end of 2019 (Advanced Television 5 Mar 2020, Tubefilter 4 Mar 2020, NextTV; secondary sources).

SOLIDQuibi lasted about 6 months

Launch 6 Apr 2020, shutdown letter 21 Oct 2020, streaming ended on or about 1 Dec 2020 (open letter; Tubefilter 23 Oct 2020, secondary source).

SOLIDKatzenberg once blamed "everything" on COVID

To the NYT on 11 May 2020: "I attribute everything that has gone wrong to coronavirus. Everything. But we own it." (via TechCrunch, Hollywood Reporter; secondary sources). In the same interview he admitted the user numbers were "not close to what we wanted".

SOLIDAt launch you could not take screenshots

Screenshots arrived only in July 2020, described by Quibi as "a sort of public beta" (MobileSyrup 24 Jul 2020, secondary source).

CAUTION"Only 72,000 people paid"

This is a Sensor Tower estimate, an upper bound, and covers only the roughly 910,000 people who signed up in the first 3 days, not total subscribers. Quibi rejected it as wrong "by an order of magnitude", yet never published its own figure (TechCrunch 8 Jul 2020, secondary source).

CAUTION"You couldn't watch Quibi on TV"

True at launch. AirPlay came in late May, Chromecast in June, TV apps in October 2020, just as the company was about to close (MobileSyrup, IPS, iMore; secondary sources).

CAUTION"Quibi stole Eko's Turnstyle technology"

This was Eko's allegation, never decided on the merits. In July 2020 the court denied a preliminary injunction because Eko had not shown "irreparable harm". The case ended on 24 Sep 2021 with a settlement: all claims dropped, Turnstyle handed to Eko, no admission of wrongdoing; reportedly no money changed hands (dockets 2:20-cv-02250 and 2:20-cv-02299; Variety via Tubefilter, Cord Cutters News; secondary sources for the terms).

CAUTION"Every minute of content cost $100,000"

Katzenberg said production cost "up to" $100,000 per minute, a ceiling, not an average (Business Insider, Tubefilter; secondary sources). First-year content spend also differs between outlets: $1 billion or $1.1 billion, planned or spent.

CAUTION"Quibi spent big without ever testing with users"

The public record shows the order: funding, a $1 billion content pledge, Super Bowl ads, and only then real users; and the features users asked for all came after launch. But there are no public documents on how Quibi tested internally, so "never tested" cannot be claimed.

CAUTION"Quibi returned $350 million to investors"

The shutdown letter says it would "return cash to our shareholders". The figure of about $350 million comes from Bloomberg and the WSJ, not from the company (secondary sources).

FALSE"Quibi failed only because of COVID"

The shutdown letter itself gives two reasons, the idea first, and suspects both. What users complained about (no TV, no screenshots) were product choices made before the pandemic. COVID was a real headwind, but not the only reason.

FALSE"Roku bought Quibi"

Roku bought exclusive global distribution rights to more than 75 shows and documentaries, not the company. Turnstyle was not part of the deal and later went to Eko (Roku press release, Business Wire 8 Jan 2021; Cord Cutters News, secondary source).

FALSE"Quibi proved short video is a bad idea"

The shutdown letter doubts "a standalone streaming service" that charges money, not short video in general. Free short video on social platforms kept growing after 2020. What failed was a specific bundle of bets, not the format.

Source: open letter 21 Oct 2020; court dockets 2:20-cv-02250 and 2:20-cv-02299; Roku press release 8 Jan 2021; the articles named in each row (secondary sources).

  • The shutdown letter names "the idea" as the first reason

  • Committed big money before any real users
  • Fixed the product after complaints, after launch
  • Never published its paying count, even while disputing others' numbers

Quibi did not die for lack of money or talent. It died because big money ran ahead of the users' answer.

More: why "it was COVID" is the most comfortable explanation

An outside cause nobody controls means nobody is responsible. That is why, after any failure, it tends to be the first explanation spoken. With Quibi, the founder himself said so in May 2020.

The more useful question is: without COVID, what would still be wrong? The app would still run only on phones, still block screenshots, still ask people to pay for 10-minute episodes while free short video was everywhere. By October 2020 the shutdown letter also put "the idea" before "timing".

Part 6

Work lesson: test with real users before spending big

For any big bet: a new product, a new course, a large campaign, hiring a new team, buying expensive software.

List assumptions, mark the riskiest in redWhich one, if wrong, sinks the whole project? Test that first, not the easiest one.
Real people, real behaviourOne person who pays, returns and tells a friend is worth more than ten who praise it in a meeting.
Money in stagesSplit the budget into 3 to 4 stages. The next one opens only when the previous one has written evidence.
Stop criteria written in advance"If after 8 weeks we have fewer than X paying users, we stop." Write it while calm, not when the money is nearly gone.
Check your own team (5 questions, 10 minutes)

1. Which assumption does your team's biggest project rest on? Write it as one sentence starting with "Users will…".

2. How many real users (not colleagues, not the boss) have touched it?

3. How much of the budget was spent before you heard from them?

4. Is the metric you report "curiosity" (views, downloads, free sign-ups) or "need" (paying, returning, referring)?

5. Have the project's stop criteria been written down, and who is allowed to say "stop"?

Quibi is not succeeding, likely for one of two reasons: because the idea itself wasn't strong enough to justify a standalone streaming service or because of our timing.Katzenberg and Whitman, open letter, 21 Oct 2020 (as quoted by Tubefilter, CFO.com, Verdict).
