AI for healthcare staff

Use AI for the paperwork side of healthcare while keeping patient information private. AI drafts; healthcare staff decide.

A learning tool, not a medical device. Not for diagnosis, orders or prescribing, and no substitute for clinical guidelines, local protocols or your health authority's rules.

Tool 1 · Video 603

De-identify patient notes before pasting them into AI

Paste your notes and the tool finds names (after words like patient, Mr, Mrs), dates of birth, ID and insurance numbers, phone numbers, emails, record and bed numbers and addresses, and swaps them for placeholders. Untick anything that doesn't need hiding, add words it missed, then copy it to the AI.

Runs on your device. Nothing you paste is sent anywhere.

Found

    It can't catch everything, especially names written in lower case or rare details (an unusual job, an event in the news). Always reread the whole de-identified text before copying it, and only use AI tools your hospital has approved.

        Tool 6 · Video 611–614
        

        Map of specialist healthcare AI tools
        

        Five kinds of AI tools found in hospitals: what each does, example products, what to check and who decides. No rankings, no prices, no endorsements.
        

        

        Video 611
        Ambient scribes
        What it does
        Listens to the consultation and drafts the clinical note in the record's format for the clinician to review and sign.
        Examples
        Microsoft Dragon Copilot, Abridge, Ambience (mostly sold to US health systems); Heidi (has a free tier for individuals)
        Check
        Patient told and consents to recording; where the data is stored; how well it handles the local language and accents.
        Who decides
        The clinician reads every line before signing. It can mishear drug names or add things the patient never said.
        Video 612
        Evidence search
        What it does
        Answers clinical questions from the published literature, with a citation for each point that opens the source.
        Examples
        OpenEvidence; AI assistants built into some clinical reference databases
        Check
        Open each citation and check it says what the summary claims; sparse topics are easy to get wrong; most evidence comes from other countries.
        Who decides
        Treatment decisions follow national guidelines and local protocols.
        Video 613
        Medical imaging
        What it does
        Marks suspicious areas on X-rays and CT and moves urgent studies up the queue. A second reader.
        Examples
        DrAid by VinBrain (Vietnam): the company says it is used in over 100 hospitals; cleared by the US FDA to help detect pneumothorax on chest X-rays
        Check
        Trust it only for the exact use it was cleared for; it is regulated as a medical device; accuracy figures are often the vendor's own.
        Who decides
        The report is signed by the radiologist.
        Video 614
        Alerts in hospital software
        What it does
        Flags drug interactions, recorded allergies and out-of-range results; newer ones predict patients at risk of deteriorating.
        Examples
        Features inside hospital information systems and electronic records (varies by vendor)
        Check
        Track how often each alert is right; if it is mostly wrong, ask for the threshold to be tuned to avoid alert fatigue.
        Who decides
        The person at the bedside who has seen and examined the patient.
        Video 601
        General chatbots for paperwork
        What it does
        Summaries, plain-language rewrites, emails, minutes, reports, revision. Not built for healthcare.
        Examples
        ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot (only versions your hospital has approved)
        Check
        De-identify before pasting; prone to inventing figures and citations (hallucination); run the 5 checks before use.
        Who decides
        A qualified person reads and takes responsibility for anything used with patients.

        Tool 7 · Video 615
        

        Six questions before your hospital adopts an AI tool
        

        Fill it in when a vendor pitches a new tool. Licence, data and accountability must all be Yes. Print it for your department committee or procurement.
        

        




        

        1Licence: is the tool approved for this exact use where you work?
        Ask for the approval number, the cleared indication and the device class (if any).
        2Data: where is patient data stored, does it leave the country, is it used for further training?
        Check against data protection law; get the vendor's commitment in writing.
        3Evidence: is there independent research, or only the vendor's own figures?
        Ask for the paper, where it was done and the sample size.
        4Local fit: has it been tested on patients and records like ours?
        Ask to trial it on 20-50 de-identified cases from your own department.
        5Integration: does it work with the hospital software you already use?
        Ask IT; check whether staff would have to enter data twice.
        6Accountability: who is responsible when the AI is wrong, and where are incidents reported?
        Put it in the contract and the hospital's incident reporting process.
