LiDAR, radar or camera: which is best?

Three eyes take the field

LiDAR uses laser light, radar uses radio waves, and a camera uses the light that's already there. Today the three eyes compete in six events to see who is good at what.

EVENTS 1-2

Distance and speed

For distance, LiDAR is the most precise, down to a few centimeters. For speed, radar wins thanks to Doppler. A camera has to compare several frames to estimate either, so it comes last in both.

EVENTS 3-4

Color, text and night

For reading color and text, the camera has no rival. But at night the camera gets much weaker, while LiDAR and radar send out their own signal, so dark or light makes no difference.

EVENTS 5-6

Fog, rain and price

In fog, radar holds up best. On price, the camera is cheapest, radar sits in the middle, and LiDAR costs the most, though its price is falling fast.

TWO SCHOOLS

Cameras only, or the full set

In the real world there are two schools. In 2021, Tesla dropped radar from new cars sold in North America to rely on cameras only. Waymo's sixth-generation robotaxi carries 13 cameras, 4 LiDARs and 6 radars.

HOW IT WORKS

Why use several kinds

Several kinds are used because they fail in different ways. When the sun blinds the camera, radar is fine; when radar mistakes a can for an obstacle, the camera sees clearly. Both failing at once is much rarer.

Find the eyes on your car

Go and look at your family car. The flat panel behind the logo is usually radar, the unit behind the rear-view mirror is a camera, and the small round dots on the bumper are ultrasonic parking sensors.
