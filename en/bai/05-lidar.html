What is LiDAR? How self-driving cars measure distance with light

In the dark of night, a self-driving car can still "see" a person walking ahead. The secret is LiDAR: measuring distance with light.

How does LiDAR work?

In the dark of night, a self-driving car can still clearly see a person walking ahead. The secret is a technology called LiDAR.

Measuring distance with light

LiDAR means measuring distance with light. It's like a bat listening for echoes, but it uses laser pulses instead of sound.

STEP 1/3

Send a laser flash

Step one: the device sends out a very short laser flash that your eyes can't see. The flash flies out, hits an object and bounces back.

STEP 2/3

Time the trip there and back

Step two: the device times how long it takes, from sending to receiving. Light is so fast that going 15 meters and back takes only one ten-millionth of a second.

STEP 3/3

Work out the distance

Step three: work out the distance. Multiply the time by the speed of light, then divide by two, because the light went there and came back.

Millions of dots

The device does this hundreds of thousands of times per second, in every direction. Each measurement is one dot. Together they make a 3D map called a point cloud.

AI AT WORK

AI reads the point cloud

Then AI reads those dots to tell what is a person, what is a car, and how far away they are. That's how the car knows when to brake.

AROUND YOU

LiDAR is all around you

LiDAR is also in robot vacuums, mapping drones and the Pro iPhones.

Try it now

Measure height with your phone

Want to try it? Open the Measure app on a Pro iPhone and point it at a person. It will show their height on its own.
