How to spot scam messages written by AI

Scam messages used to be full of spelling mistakes. Now scammers use AI too: in seconds they have a hundred smooth messages using your real name and workplace. So stop checking spelling, and check what the message asks you to do.

Messages that look too right

Scam messages used to be full of spelling mistakes, so you could spot them right away. Now they read just like a real bank.

WHY

Scammers use AI too

Because scammers use AI too. In a few seconds they have a hundred smooth messages, even using your real name and where you work.

DROP THE OLD TRICK

Stop checking the spelling

So stop looking for spelling mistakes. Look at what the message asks you to do.

RED FLAGS

Three dangerous requests

Three red flags: asking you to click a link to log in, asking for a one-time code (OTP), or asking you to install a strange app. Real banks don't do that by text.

LET AI CHECK

Use AI against AI

A good trick: paste the message into AI, hide your personal details, and ask: does this message show signs of a scam? AI points out red flags very quickly. But the final call is still yours: call the official hotline to ask.

GO THERE YOURSELF

Don't click links

To check your account, open the bank app you already have installed. Never click links in a text message.

Try it now

Check one message

Find a suspicious message on your phone, ask AI to point out the red flags, then delete it.
