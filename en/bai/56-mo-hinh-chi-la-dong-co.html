What makes up an AI product? The model is just the engine

Why do two apps using the same AI model end up so different, one you use daily and one you delete after a try?

Watch videoVideo in Vietnamese

Same model, two different fates

Two apps use the same AI model. One you use every day, the other you delete after one try. Why? Because the model is just the engine.

An engine isn't a car

A good engine isn't a car yet. To drive, you also need fuel, a steering wheel, brakes and a dashboard. An AI product is the same.

FUEL

Your own data

The fuel is your own data. A bakery's chatbot has to know that bakery's menu, prices and opening hours, not just general knowledge from the internet.

STEERING WHEEL

The interface

The steering wheel is the interface: buttons, suggested questions, a place to show results. A good interface helps people know what to ask, and what to do with the answer.

BRAKES

Guardrails

The brakes are guardrails: things AI must not say or do. The bakery chatbot must not promise discounts on its own, or chat about unrelated topics.

DASHBOARD

Feedback from users

And the dashboard: like and dislike buttons, questions people have to ask twice. It tells the product team whether the car is running well, or where it's about to break.

Where's the difference?

Many companies can rent the same engine. What makes the difference is the other four parts.

Try it now

Check an AI app you use

Open an AI app you use often and ask yourself: what does it know about my own work? What does it block? Does it let me rate its answers? You'll see where it's strong and weak.
