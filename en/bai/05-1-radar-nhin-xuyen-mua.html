How radar helps cars see through fog

Thick fog scatters LiDAR's laser, so a car needs a second eye: radar, which measures both distance and speed.

Picking up from LiDAR

Part 1, video 5 on LiDAR, measured the road in three steps: fire a laser pulse, start the clock, work out the distance. But in thick fog, water droplets scatter the laser everywhere, and the car needs a second eye: radar.

HOW IT WORKS

Long waves slip through fog

Radar measures distance with radio waves. The waves used in cars are almost 4 mm long, about 4,300 times longer than LiDAR's laser. Fog droplets are far smaller than that, so the waves slip right through.

THREE STEPS

The same three steps as LiDAR

Radar follows exactly the same three steps as LiDAR: send a wave, time it, work out the distance. For a car 150 meters ahead, the round trip takes just one millionth of a second.

ONE MORE STEP

Doppler measures speed

Radar's big advantage is measuring speed instantly, thanks to the Doppler effect. Just as an ambulance siren sounds higher as it comes closer, waves bouncing off an approaching object get squeezed together. If the car ahead is 10 meters per second slower, the wave shifts by about 5,000 hertz.

LIMITS

Blurry, and easily fooled

The trade-off: radar sees very blurrily, so it struggles to tell a person from a motorbike or a sign. Metal reflects very strongly, so a can or an overpass can be mistaken for an obstacle.

AROUND YOU

Radar is everywhere

Radar powers adaptive cruise control, blind-spot warnings and weather rain maps.

Try it now

Look at rain radar

Open the rain map in your weather app and pick the radar layer. The green, yellow and red patches are waves bouncing back off raindrops, showing whether rain is about to reach you.
