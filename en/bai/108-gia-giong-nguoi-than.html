AI voice cloning scams: how to spot them and stay safe

Midnight, your "son" calls asking for $800 right away, and the voice sounds exactly right. But it could be a voice clone: AI copying a voice from a short recording taken from a video you posted.

A call in the middle of the night

Midnight, the phone rings. Her son's voice: Mom, I'm in trouble, send me $800 right now. The voice sounds exactly the same, but the caller isn't her son.

VOICE CLONE

Copying a voice from a short recording

That's a voice clone: AI copying a voice. Scammers only need a short recording, taken from a video you posted online, to copy a voice.

WARNING SIGNS

Three signs

Watch for three signs: they rush you, they ask you to send money or read out a one-time code (OTP), and they tell you not to tell anyone.

WHAT TO DO

Hang up and call back yourself

The surest way: hang up, then call back yourself on the number you already have saved. Don't call back the number that just called.

TEST THEM

Something only family knows

Or ask something only family would know, like what everyone had for dinner yesterday. A scammer can't answer.

PREVENT IT

Share less of your voice

Prevent it ahead of time: post fewer public videos with voices, especially of older family members and young kids.

Try it now

Send this to your parents

Send this video to your parents, and agree on a rule: for urgent money matters, always call back to check.
