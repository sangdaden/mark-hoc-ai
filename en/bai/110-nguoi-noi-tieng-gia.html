Celebrity investment offers online: real, or deepfake?

A video ad: a famous businessman explains how to invest for 30% a month. The face is right, the voice is right, but he never said it: scammers took a real interview and used AI to change the lips and voice.

A celebrity inviting you to invest?

Mark is scrolling and sees a video ad: a famous businessman explains how to invest for 30% profit a month. The face is right, the voice is right. But that person never said this.

HOW IT'S DONE

Pieced together from old videos

Scammers take a real interview video, use AI to change the lip movements and dub in a cloned voice, and turn it into a completely fake invitation.

WARNING SIGNS

High returns, guaranteed, urgent

Common signs: high returns that are "guaranteed", only a few spots left, and an invitation to a private chat group for "guidance".

LOOK CLOSELY

Lip movements and the page

Look closely: the lips don't match the sound, and the page running the ad is usually new, not that person's official page.

CHECK IT

Look on the official page

Check it: go to that person's official page, or read trusted news. A real invitation never lives only in some strange ad.

REPORT IT

Help others

When you see a fake ad, report it. You might save an older person from being scammed.

Try it now

Remember three words

Remember that high returns, guarantees and urgency are three familiar signs of a scam, then share this with your family.
