How do multi-agent systems work? Orchestrator, subagents and reviewer

An AI team splits roles just like a newsroom.

Follow on YouTubeComing soonVideo in Vietnamese

Like a newsroom

A newsroom has an editor-in-chief, reporters and proofreaders. Multi-agent splits roles the same way.

ORCHESTRATOR

Give clear assignments

The orchestrator takes the job from you, breaks it down, and writes clear instructions for each subagent: what to find, where, and in what format. Vague instructions mean subagents end up doing the same work twice.

SUBAGENT

Each worker has its own toolkit

The subagents are specialists. Each has exactly the tools it needs: one searches the web, one reads spreadsheets, one writes.

REVIEWER

Another pair of eyes

Add a critic agent, called a reviewer: it writes nothing, it just looks for mistakes, checks sources and points out gaps. Two different pairs of eyes catch errors one would miss.

PUTTING IT TOGETHER

One result for you

Finally, the orchestrator puts everything together into one result for you.

A SHARED LANGUAGE

MCP and A2A

Agents talking to each other need shared standards too. MCP lets agents plug into tools. And A2A, short for Agent to Agent, is the standard for one company's agent to hand work to another company's agent.

Try it now

Your own team of two

Open two chat windows. Window one writes the article. Window two plays a tough critic. Pass the article back and forth twice, and you'll see the later version is much better.
