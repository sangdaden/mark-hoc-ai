Fooled even on a video call? How to spot deepfakes in video calls

A friend messages to borrow money. Mark video calls, sees his friend's face for a few seconds, the connection drops, and he sends the money. But the account was hacked, and the face was a live deepfake (see also video 14).

You saw the face and still got fooled

A close friend messages asking to borrow money. Mark is careful and video calls, sees his friend's face for a few seconds, then the connection drops. Mark feels safe and sends the money. But the account was hacked, and that face was a deepfake.

DEEPFAKE

A fake face during the call

Deepfakes can now run live during a call, putting someone else's face over the scammer's, as in video 14.

WARNING SIGNS

Short, blurry, dropped

Signs: the call is very short, the picture is blurry or choppy, then they blame a bad connection to hang up and switch to texting.

TEST FOR A REAL PERSON

Turn sideways, wave a hand across the face

If you're suspicious, ask them to turn their face to the side, or wave a hand in front of their face. Fake faces often warp at those moments. But the technology keeps getting better, so don't rely on your eyes alone.

THE RULE

Check through another channel

The golden rule: check through another channel. Call the phone number you have saved, or ask a friend you both know.

IF IT HAPPENED

Report it right away

If you already sent money, call your bank right away to ask them to stop the transaction, and report it to the police. The faster, the better your chances.

Try it now

Message your family group

Message your family group: anyone asking to borrow money online, even on a video call, must be called back on their phone number to confirm.
