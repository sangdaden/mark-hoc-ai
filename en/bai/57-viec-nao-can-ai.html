Which tasks should use AI, and which shouldn't? 2 filter questions

Holding a hammer, everything looks like a nail. With AI it's the same: not every task is worth giving to AI.

Watch videoVideo in Vietnamese

Holding a hammer, everything looks like a nail

With AI in hand, you want to give it every task. It's like holding a hammer: everything looks like a nail. But not every task is worth it.

TWO QUESTIONS

Two filter questions

Before you start, ask two questions. One: if AI gets it wrong once, what happens? Two: is it easy to check its work?

DO IT NOW

AI's sweet spot

Drafting an email, summing up a meeting, naming a new cake. If it's wrong, you fix a few words, and one glance tells you if it's right. This is AI's sweet spot.

CAREFUL

The red zone: AI helps, people decide

But calculating salaries, diagnosing illness, signing contracts for you: one mistake costs money or health, and checking it almost means redoing it from scratch. Here, AI should only help, and a person must decide.

NO AI NEEDED

When a formula is enough

Some tasks don't need AI at all. Calculating tax with a fixed formula? A spreadsheet is enough: faster, cheaper and right every time.

RECAP

Put it all in one table

Put it all in one table: small mistakes that are easy to check, do it now. Big mistakes that are hard to check, avoid. In between, let AI draft and a person approve.

Try it now

Score your own tasks

Write down three tasks you do each week, and score each with those two questions. Hand the ones in the "do it now" box to AI first.

PART 3

AI still makes mistakes sometimes

You've picked the right tasks. But AI still gets things wrong sometimes. How do you design so those mistakes don't cause harm? See you in part 3.
