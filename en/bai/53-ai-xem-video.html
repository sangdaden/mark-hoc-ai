How does AI watch video?

Last time, AI listened and talked with you like a real person. Now give it an hour-long video. A few seconds later, there's a summary. How does it watch?

FRAMES

A video is a stack of photos

A video is really a stack of still pictures, called frames, flipped very fast. Usually about 30 per second, with a soundtrack running alongside.

SAMPLING

Only a few frames per second

One hour of video is about 108,000 pictures. Watching them all is too heavy. So AI only takes samples: a few frames per second, sometimes just 1.

SPEECH

It listens to the speech too

At the same time, AI transcribes what's said into text, the same way it heard you in part 1. Frames and sentences all have timestamps, so AI knows what happened at which minute.

PUTTING IT TOGETHER

Pictures plus words make the main idea

Putting pictures and words together, AI gets the main content: who's talking, what they're doing, which slide is on the screen.

WEAK SPOT

Quick details get missed

But because it only skims, something that happens in half a second can slip between two frames. A lightning-fast goal, a small line of text flashing by. AI may miss it, or even guess.
