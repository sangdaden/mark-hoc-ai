How to design AI products when AI can be wrong: a 4-step ladder

An AI that's right 95% of the time sounds great, but it's still wrong 1 time in 20. Where does that mistake land?

Watch videoVideo in Vietnamese

Is 95% right good enough?

An AI that's right ninety-five percent of the time sounds great. But one time in twenty, it's wrong. Product makers have to plan ahead: where does that mistake land?

A four-step ladder

Picture a ladder with four steps, from AI only suggesting to AI doing everything itself.

STEP 1

Suggest

Step one: suggest. Gray text appears as you type. Tap to accept, ignore it if you don't want it. If AI is wrong, no harm done.

STEP 2

Draft

Step two: draft. AI writes the whole letter, and you read it, edit it, then send.

STEP 3

Ask to confirm

Step three: AI gets everything ready, but before paying or booking, it asks: "Do you confirm?".

STEP 4

Do it alone

Step four: AI just does it, like a spam filter. This only fits when mistakes are easy to undo: an email filtered by mistake can still be recovered.

THE RULE

Hard to undo, stay low

The rule is: the harder a mistake is to undo, the lower the step should be. Plus three good habits: show sources, let users correct things, and be willing to say "I'm not sure".

Try it now

Which step is your app on?

For each AI app you use, ask: which step is it on? And if it's wrong, is there an undo button?
