Scam field guide

The 8 most common AI scams

Fake voice of a child asking for money

AI copies a voice from a short clip online.

Red flags

  • Late-night call, panicked voice, an accident or arrest story
  • Unknown number, or “I lost my phone”
  • Pay now, and keep it secret

Do this

  • Hang up, call the number you know
  • Ask for the family safe word

Video call with a familiar face

A friend's account is hijacked; the face on the call is a deepfake.

Red flags

  • A few seconds, blurry, then the call drops
  • Asks you to pay an account in another name
  • Avoids turning their head when asked

Do this

  • Call back on their old phone number
  • Wrong account name means stop

Fake celebrity investment ads

Real footage is lip-synced and re-voiced by AI, then leads to an “AI trading” app.

Red flags

  • Steady daily or monthly profit, guaranteed
  • A small first withdrawal works, then you're locked out
  • Demands more “fees” or “taxes” before you can withdraw

Do this

  • Never pay more to withdraw
  • Search the celebrity's name with “fake”

Polished scam texts written by AI

No more typos; they use your name and your workplace.

Red flags

  • Threatens to lock your account or fine you
  • Includes a link to tap or a file to install
  • Asks for an OTP code or password

Do this

  • Judge what it asks you to do, not the spelling
  • Open your bank app yourself to check

Fake doctors selling medicine

Real doctors' and stars' faces and voices are pasted into medicine ads.

Red flags

  • Promises to cure a chronic illness
  • Sold only through chat, cash on delivery
  • Discount for today only

Do this

  • Ask your own doctor first
  • Send this page to your parents

Easy jobs, high pay, jobs abroad

From “top-up tasks” to being trafficked into scam compounds abroad.

Red flags

  • High pay for liking posts or confirming orders
  • You must pay before you can start
  • A free ticket abroad, no contract

Do this

  • Never pay to get a job
  • Tell your family before you go

Fake apps that read your texts

Fake apps ask to read your texts so they can grab bank OTP codes.

Red flags

  • Installed from a link, not an app store
  • Asks for SMS or accessibility access
  • Someone on the phone guides the install

Do this

  • Remove it, call your bank to freeze the account
  • Install only from Google Play or the App Store

“Get your money back” services

Victims get scammed twice by people posing as lawyers or police.

Red flags

  • They find you right after you were scammed
  • Promise to recover everything, fast
  • Ask for a fee up front

Do this

  • Deal only with your real bank and the police
  • Never pay a fee to recover money

Already scammed

Sent money or shared an OTP? Do this now, in order

113Police (Vietnam)Card hotlineYour bankcanhbao.khonggianmang.vnReport online scams (Vietnam)
  1. Stop every transaction. Don't send more, even if they say a fee will get your money back.
  2. Call your bank right away (number on your card or in the app). Give the time, amount and receiving account, and ask them to freeze it.
  3. Keep evidence: screenshots of messages, call history, receipts. Don't delete anything.
  4. Report it to the police. In Vietnam, call 113 or go to your local police station.
  5. Change your passwords, warn family and friends (scammers may use your name next), and in Vietnam report it at canhbao.khonggianmang.vn.

Your answers stay on your device; nothing is sent. This page helps you spot warning signs; it doesn't replace your bank or the police. When in doubt, ask them directly.
